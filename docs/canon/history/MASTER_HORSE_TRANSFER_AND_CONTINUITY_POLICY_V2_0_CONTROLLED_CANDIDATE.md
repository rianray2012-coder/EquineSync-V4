# 1. Constitutional Purpose

If separately founder-adopted, this document will establish the controlling EquineSync policy for preserving a horse's durable identity and governed history while the relationships, people, organizations, facilities, providers, accounts, and operational circumstances surrounding that horse change. Until then, it is a reviewed candidate and the recorded FD01 decision plus existing locked canons remain controlling.

It exists to answer the platform-level questions that arise whenever a horse is sold, gifted, moved, temporarily placed, hospitalized, returned, adopted, fostered, transferred between facilities, transferred between trainers, or otherwise placed under a new relationship or care context:

1. What must remain permanently associated with the same real-world horse?
2. Which relationships are changing, and which are not?
3. Who is authorized to request, approve, confirm, review, or challenge the change?
4. What evidence supports the requested change?
5. When does the change become effective for each relevant purpose?
6. Which permissions must be removed, recalculated, granted, or preserved?
7. Which records follow the horse, which remain with their author or steward, and which require restricted projection?
8. What must former parties retain for lawful, contractual, operational, safety, or evidentiary purposes?
9. What happens when downstream systems are incomplete, unavailable, disputed, or offline?
10. How does EquineSync preserve history without presenting itself as a court, title registry, lien tribunal, or legal decision-maker?

This candidate defines proposed enduring product policy and constitutional boundaries. Neither review nor later adoption would itself approve any implementation batch, runtime behavior, migration, permission change, external-service activation, production mutation, or public launch.

# 2. Constitutional Mission

EquineSync shall preserve the continuous, attributable, auditable identity and governed history of each horse throughout the horse's lifetime and archival lifecycle while supporting safe, accurate, and reviewable transitions among authorized owners, custodians, facilities, trainers, providers, guardians, representatives, and organizations.

The platform shall be designed to protect:

- the horse's welfare and continuity of care;
- the integrity of the horse's canonical identity;
- the distinction among ownership, custody, possession, care, payment, training, facility, and authority relationships;
- the accuracy and provenance of transfer evidence;
- the privacy and lawful interests of current and former participants;
- the durability of authorship, stewardship, and audit history;
- the ability to correct error without rewriting history;
- the ability to stop, defer, or route unsupported cases without inventing legal conclusions.

EquineSync shall assist authorized people and organizations in documenting and administering horse continuity. It shall not replace legal authority or adjudicate contested legal rights.

# 5. Defined Terms

For purposes of this canon:

## 5.1 Canonical horse identity

The durable EquineSync identity assigned to a real-world horse. The identifier remains stable while names, owners, facilities, accounts, records, and other attributes change.

A canonical horse identity is a platform identity, not a government-issued title and not proof of legal ownership.

## 5.2 Horse Passport

The governed longitudinal projection of identity, provenance, relationships, continuity information, and authorized historical facts associated with the canonical horse.

The Passport is not one unrestricted document. It is a policy-controlled projection whose contents vary according to authority, purpose, relationship, privacy, sensitivity, record classification, jurisdiction, and current restrictions.

## 5.3 Transfer

A governed event or process that changes one or more relationships, authority contexts, access rights, operational responsibilities, custody facts, possession facts, or continuity projections associated with a horse.

A transfer does not always change ownership. A barn move, trainer change, hospitalization, temporary custody placement, or emergency evacuation may be a transfer event without an ownership transfer.

## 5.4 Ownership relationship

A recorded relationship asserting or verifying that a person or organization has an ownership interest in a horse. EquineSync must preserve the source, authority basis, confidence, effective period, limitations, and dispute state of the relationship.

The label must not be presented as an unqualified legal conclusion when the underlying evidence is asserted, imported, incomplete, or disputed.

## 5.5 Custody

Responsibility for the horse's immediate care, control, or safekeeping during a defined period. Custody does not independently establish ownership.

## 5.6 Possession

The factual or asserted physical control or location of the horse at a point or during a period. Possession does not independently establish ownership, payment responsibility, or transfer authority.

## 5.7 Transfer case

The governed container that preserves the request, participants, authorities, evidence, decisions, notices, state transitions, exceptions, restrictions, and downstream acknowledgements for a transfer.

## 5.8 Effective time

A time at which a particular relationship, authority, possession fact, operational duty, or system consequence becomes effective. Different effective times may apply to different aspects of the same transfer.

## 5.9 Completion

The state reached after all required critical impacts, acknowledgements, audits, and exception checks have satisfied the applicable policy. Completion is not synonymous with approval or legal effect.

## 5.10 Former party

A person or organization whose current relationship has ended or materially changed, including a former owner, barn, trainer, custodian, provider, representative, payer, or guardian.

## 5.11 Evidence

A document, signature, record, registry result, attestation, scan, identifier, communication, system event, or other attributed source used to support, challenge, route, or review a transfer.

## 5.12 Restriction

A scoped, reasoned, time-bound or review-bound limitation on transfer, access, visibility, communication, authority, or downstream effect. A restriction must not silently become a permanent ownership conclusion.

# 6. Constitutional Principles

## 6.1 One real-world horse should converge to one canonical identity

EquineSync shall seek to maintain one durable canonical identity for each real-world horse.

This principle does not permit automatic merging. Duplicate candidates may temporarily coexist while identity is investigated. Candidate linking, merge, unmerge, correction, and fraud review are separate governed actions.

## 6.2 Canonical identity is durable; attributes are changeable

The canonical identifier must remain stable through:

- name changes;
- ownership changes;
- barn and trainer changes;
- account closure;
- organization changes;
- temporary placements;
- medical episodes;
- retirement;
- death and memorial state;
- correction of descriptive attributes;
- external-registry changes.

Durability does not mean every data element is immutable or retained forever. Attributes may be corrected, restricted, minimized, erased where lawful, superseded, or disposed of under the Record Stewardship and Retention Model.

## 6.3 History is preserved through non-destructive correction

A transfer must not be erased merely because it was later reversed, challenged, superseded, or corrected.

The platform should preserve:

- what was asserted;
- what evidence was presented;
- what decision was made;
- when the decision was effective;
- what later correction occurred;
- who authorized the correction;
- which downstream effects occurred.

Preservation remains subject to lawful erasure, retention limits, minimization, privilege, legal hold, and security policy. The constitutional rule is against silent historical rewriting, not against lawful data governance.

## 6.4 Relationship types remain distinct

The system must separately model, where applicable:

- asserted ownership;
- verified ownership;
- beneficial interest;
- legal representation;
- fiduciary authority;
- guardianship;
- physical custody;
- possession;
- facility assignment;
- trainer responsibility;
- veterinary or provider responsibility;
- payer responsibility;
- emergency contact;
- Care Circle participation;
- record stewardship;
- account access.

No one relationship silently proves or grants another.

## 6.5 Accounts do not define horse truth

A horse's identity and history must not depend on whether any participant has an active EquineSync account.

A person or organization may exist as a pending, external, unverified, historical, represented, or non-login principal. Account activation may be required for direct application access, but not for preserving accurate historical or relationship facts.

## 6.6 Permissions follow present authority and governed historical purpose

Former ownership, former custody, authorship, payment, account creation, or possession does not create perpetual access.

Access must be recalculated from:

- current identity and authentication;
- current relationship and representation context;
- record classification and stewardship;
- purpose;
- consent;
- claims and restrictions;
- historical-access rules;
- legal and contractual obligations;
- minimum necessary projection.

## 6.7 Automation assists; governed authority decides

Automation may:

- check completeness;
- validate format;
- verify cryptographic or provider status;
- compare identifiers;
- identify conflicts;
- detect duplicates;
- calculate confidence;
- route cases;
- recommend review;
- issue notices;
- reconcile authorized downstream effects.

Automation shall not independently determine contested legal ownership, fiduciary power, guardian power, lien validity, court authority, or the legal effect of disputed evidence.

## 6.8 Unsupported cases fail safely

When a transfer type, authority class, evidence type, jurisdiction, or downstream dependency is not authorized, the system must use an explicit safe outcome:

- `UNSUPPORTED_TRANSFER_TYPE`;
- `DEFERRED_BY_POLICY`;
- `REQUIRES_SPECIALIST_REVIEW`;
- `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`;
- `REQUIRES_FUTURE_RF`;
- `DENIED_BY_DEFAULT`.

The system must not approximate unsupported legal behavior or silently choose a policy default that has not been approved.

## 6.9 Horse welfare may justify emergency action, not permanent authority

Emergency workflows may support minimum necessary action to protect life, health, safety, evacuation, or infectious-disease control.

Emergency movement, treatment, custody, or access does not by itself create permanent ownership, unlimited access, or enduring authority.

## 6.10 Transfer truth and display are separate

A historical fact may be preserved while withheld, summarized, redacted, or differently projected to a particular viewer.

EquineSync must not confuse:

- data preservation;
- record stewardship;
- ordinary user visibility;
- legal production;
- audit access;
- former-party access;
- provider access;
- public memorial display.

# 7. The Horse Passport

## 7.1 Constitutional role

The Horse Passport is the continuity spine for the horse. It is intended to preserve authorized identity and longitudinal context while surrounding relationships change.

The Passport may include governed projections of:

- canonical identifiers;
- names and prior names;
- signalment and descriptive characteristics;
- strong external identifiers and their issuers;
- ownership and custody timeline summaries;
- facility and trainer timeline summaries;
- clinically necessary continuity information;
- safety restrictions;
- provider relationships;
- lifecycle events;
- memorial status;
- transfer history;
- provenance and verification status.

## 7.2 Passport is not a universal visibility bundle

No participant is entitled to every Passport-associated record merely because the participant has access to the horse.

Passport projection must remain subject to:

- viewer identity;
- authority;
- relationship;
- consent;
- record classification;
- purpose;
- jurisdiction;
- legal hold;
- privacy restriction;
- guardian or protected-person restrictions;
- claims and dispute state;
- field-level and attachment-level controls.

## 7.3 Passport is not legal title

The Passport may record attributed ownership assertions and verified relationship states. It must not be marketed, displayed, or relied upon as a government title certificate unless a future legally authorized program expressly creates that status.

## 7.4 Passport continuity after account or organization change

The Passport must survive:

- user account closure;
- email change;
- organization departure;
- barn closure;
- provider departure;
- subscription lapse;
- business succession;
- integration disconnection;
- migration;
- archival transition.

Survival does not imply perpetual access by the departed party.

Examples include:

- barn move;
- trainer change;
- temporary custody;
- veterinary hospitalization;
- rehabilitation placement;
- emergency boarding;
- quarantine;
- hauling custody;
- show or clinic custody;
- foster placement;
- evacuation placement.

## 8.4 Lifecycle and archival events

Death, memorial state, archival transition, and lawful disposal are not ownership transfers. They may end or alter active relationships while preserving governed history.

# 10. Transfer Participants and Authority

## 10.1 Participant roles

A transfer case may include:

- initiating party;
- outgoing party;
- incoming party;
- current custodian;
- receiving custodian;
- co-owner;
- guardian;
- fiduciary;
- authorized representative;
- organization representative;
- facility;
- trainer;
- provider;
- payer;
- claims participant;
- identity reviewer;
- specialist reviewer;
- platform administrator.

Participation, notice, authority, approval, and access are separate concepts.

## 10.2 Authority must be explicit and scoped

Authority must identify, where applicable:

- the principal for whom the actor is acting;
- the source of authority;
- the scope of authority;
- the permitted action;
- the applicable horse or interest;
- the effective period;
- conditions and limitations;
- verification status;
- revocation or expiration behavior;
- conflict or dispute status.

## 10.3 Dual control for high-impact actions (recorded FD02)

FD02 is `ACCEPT_WITH_MODIFICATION`. No single human actor, account, role,
organization membership, automated system, administrator credential, or
authenticated session may unilaterally perform a high-impact action that changes
ownership authority, permanent relationship authority, canonical horse identity,
another party's access, protected history, a completed transfer, a high-impact
restriction, disputed-case treatment, duplicate identity treatment, or reversal
of a prior effective action.

Two confirmations require two distinct, verified, appropriately authorized human
actors. One person switching roles, accounts, organizations, or sessions; shared
credentials; a generic organization login; one actor representing both sides;
or automation cannot satisfy dual control. A conflicted dual representative must
route to another authorized representative, independent specialist review, or a
pending/unsupported state.

### 10.3.1 Pattern A - Bilateral Party Confirmation

Use for ordinary FD01 responsibility or relationship transitions between two
identifiable parties. The authorized outgoing or initiating party and the
authorized incoming or receiving party must confirm the same immutable transfer
version, horse, relationship change, material evidence manifest, and effective
terms. Receiving-party acceptance confirms willingness and duties; it does not
prove the outgoing party's authority.

Planning mappings are:

| FD01 class | Required bilateral confirmation |
| --- | --- |
| Sole-owner sale | Authorized outgoing owner plus incoming owner |
| Sole-owner gift | Authorized donor plus recipient |
| Verified rescue adoption | Authorized rescue representative plus adopter |
| Agreed return to prior owner | Current authorized party plus receiving prior owner |
| Barn or facility move | Authorized horse controller plus receiving facility |
| Trainer change | Authorized horse controller plus receiving trainer |
| Temporary boarding or custody | Authorized current party plus receiving facility/custodian |
| Veterinary hospitalization | Authorized treatment/custody actor plus receiving provider |
| Rehabilitation or quarantine placement | Authorized horse controller plus receiving facility/provider/custodian |
| Foster placement | Authorized organization/owner plus receiving custodian |
| Return from foster | Authorized foster custodian plus receiving owner/organization |
| Emergency boarding | Reserved to FD15; no bypass is authorized by FD02 |

Outgoing barns, former trainers, payers, account creators, invoice recipients,
and record authors may have notice, claim, authorship, retention, checkout, or
dispute rights without ownership-transfer veto authority.

### 10.3.2 Pattern B - Authorized Actor Plus Independent Specialist

Use, only when the capability is separately authorized, when an initiating actor
has authority but legal, identity, safety, claims, privacy, security, or permission
risk requires an independent specialist. Reserved examples include fiduciary,
guardian, succession, court, lien, reversal, disputed override, fraud, restricted
contact, protected-party, exceptional override, high-impact restriction, and
emergency retrospective review. Specialist classes require later definition and
may include claims, identity, legal operations, trust and safety, security,
privacy, or platform governance. An ordinary administrator is not a specialist
by title alone.

### 10.3.3 Pattern C - Independent Two-Reviewer Control

Use, only when separately authorized, for canonical identity, broad historical,
cross-tenant, or unusually consequential restriction actions. It requires one
qualified specialist and a second independent elevated reviewer with a compatible
control role. Duplicate merge/unmerge, cross-tenant repair, dispute override,
multi-history correction, platform-wide restriction, broad break-glass action,
and exceptional reversal remain excluded under FD01. At least one future duplicate
reviewer must be horse-identity qualified; the other must be qualified in platform
governance, stewardship, claims, security, or equivalent control.

### 10.3.4 Confirmation integrity and invalidation

Each confirmation binds to canonical horse, transfer case and immutable version,
subtype, principal and actor identities, authority source/scope, policy and evidence
versions, effective terms, timestamp, authentication strength, conflicts, status,
and expiration/revalidation rule. It is attributed evidence, never a Boolean alone.

A material change to horse identity, parties, principal, type, interest, custody,
effective date, material evidence, governed consideration, authority, claim,
restriction, receiving custodian/facility/trainer, or decision-affecting policy
invalidates affected confirmations. A future policy must define and audit the
narrow non-material-change rule.

### 10.3.5 Validity, emergency, and fallback

FD17 and FD18 will set duration and invitation rules. Until then, confirmation
expires with authority, revocation, material version change, changed claims or
restrictions, or failed effect-time revalidation. Reissued invitations do not
preserve confirmation silently.

FD02 does not resolve FD15. Emergency bypass remains unavailable except through
an unsupported, specialist, or manual-review path. Any later exception must be
narrow, welfare-based, time-limited, minimum necessary, reason-coded, audited,
retrospectively reviewed, and unable to create permanent ownership, unrestricted
history, or permanent receiving-party acceptance.

Missing, invalid, expired, conflicted, withdrawn, or stale confirmation prevents
effect and uses an explicit state such as `AWAITING_OUTGOING_CONFIRMATION`,
`AWAITING_INCOMING_CONFIRMATION`, `AWAITING_SPECIALIST_REVIEW`,
`AWAITING_SECOND_REVIEWER`, `CONFIRMATION_EXPIRED`,
`CONFIRMATION_WITHDRAWN`, `CONFIRMATION_INVALIDATED`,
`CONFIRMATION_CONFLICT`, `CONFIRMATION_AUTHORITY_EXPIRED`,
`DEFERRED_BY_DEPENDENCY`, `REQUIRES_SPECIALIST_REVIEW`, or
`DENIED_BY_DEFAULT`. Silence, delivery/opening, account creation, payment,
possession, upload, role, membership, invoice state, legacy fields, prior
transfers, convenience, and AI output cannot supply confirmation.

### 10.3.6 Actions outside automatic dual control

Drafting, evidence upload, non-material draft correction, withdrawal of one's
unapproved request, notice acknowledgement, already-authorized viewing,
non-authoritative completeness checks, factual observation without authority
effect, and non-submittable offline drafts may remain single-authority only when
later policy permits. Authentication, permission, audit, classification, and
authority checks still apply.

## 10.4 Administrators are not universal decision-makers

An organization administrator may administer that organization's ordinary operations. The role does not automatically confer authority to decide legal ownership, fiduciary capacity, guardian authority, lien validity, cross-tenant identity merge, or platform-wide restriction.

Specialist authority must be separately defined.

# 11. Evidence and Trust

## 11.1 Evidence does not equal authority by itself

Possession of a document, image, signature envelope, registry result, payment receipt, or legacy database value does not automatically establish the legal or operational conclusion asserted by the document.

Evidence must be evaluated within an approved policy for the transfer type.

## 11.2 Evidence metadata

Transfer evidence should preserve:

- source;
- issuer;
- submitter;
- subject;
- related horse;
- retrieval or upload time;
- document or event time;
- verification method;
- confidence or trust state;
- expiry;
- revocation state;
- jurisdiction;
- hash or integrity evidence where applicable;
- redaction state;
- retention class;
- legal-hold state;
- relationship to the decision.

## 11.3 External sources

Registries, veterinarians, insurers, e-signature providers, identity providers, competition organizations, courts, government agencies, and migration sources may contribute attributed evidence.

No external source may silently overwrite canonical truth. External evidence must remain attributed and must pass the applicable promotion, discrepancy, and revocation rules.

## 11.4 Evidence conflicts

Conflicting strong identifiers, competing ownership assertions, revoked authority, inconsistent signatures, or unexplained provenance must stop automated effect and route the case to the approved review path.

# 12. Transfer Lifecycle

## 12.1 Required state separation

The system must distinguish at least:

- draft;
- submitted;
- evidence pending;
- participant confirmation pending;
- specialist review pending;
- approved;
- rejected;
- scheduled;
- effective;
- reconciling;
- partially completed;
- completed;
- expired;
- withdrawn;
- disputed;
- restricted;
- reversed;
- corrected;
- archived.

Lower-order implementation may add states but must not collapse materially different legal, operational, or access consequences into one label.

## 12.2 Approval is not effect

Approval means the case has satisfied the approved decision standard. It does not necessarily mean the relationship has changed, the horse has moved, access has changed, or downstream systems have completed.

## 12.3 Effect is not completion

A transfer may become effective after all access-critical and safety-critical safeguards succeed while non-critical reconciliation continues.

The user interface must clearly disclose whether a transfer is approved, scheduled, effective, reconciling, partially completed, or complete.

## 12.4 Expiry and reinitiation

Expiry must not delete the transfer case. A later attempt should create a new version or case linked to the prior attempt, with fresh authority, evidence, policy, and participant validation.

* owner-supplied records;
* barn observations;
* imported records;
* AI-extracted information;
* unverified claims;
* disputed information.

Transfer does not erase the original author, source, date, confidence, restrictions, or evidentiary status.

EquineSync must not falsely represent that every provider record is automatically owned by or transferable by the horse owner. Professional-record, licensing, confidentiality, and jurisdictional rules must be respected.

Where a complete record cannot lawfully transfer, EquineSync should preserve an appropriate continuity summary, source reference, or request pathway when permitted.

---

# FD12 — PRIVATE NOTES

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Private staff, trainer, provider, barn, legal, safeguarding, personnel, risk-management, and internal operational notes do not automatically transfer with the horse.

A record must be classified according to its purpose, author, subject, expected confidentiality, legal status, safety relevance, and transferability.

The following generally must not automatically transfer:

* employee performance notes;
* internal staffing commentary;
* attorney-client communications;
* attorney work product;
* insurer or litigation strategy;
* confidential business methods;
* private billing-collection strategy;
* safeguarding reporter identities where protected;
* privileged communications;
* internal security notes;
* subjective commentary unrelated to horse care;
* draft or abandoned assessments;
* personal notes not adopted into the horse’s care record.

However, information necessary to prevent material harm must not be concealed merely because it originated in a private note.

When a private note contains material horse-care or safety information, EquineSync should support a controlled process to create a transferable factual summary that:

* separates fact from opinion;
* minimizes unrelated personal information;
* preserves provenance;
* protects privileged or confidential content;
* identifies uncertainty;
* records who prepared the summary.

---

# FD13 — UNPAID BALANCES

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

An unpaid balance does not, by itself, determine legal ownership or automatically prevent a transfer.

EquineSync must not permit a barn, trainer, provider, marketplace participant, or other creditor to unilaterally convert ordinary unpaid debt into ownership authority.

Unpaid balances may:

* remain associated with the responsible debtor;
* remain collectible after transfer;
* trigger notices, account restrictions, or dispute workflows;
* be disclosed to authorized parties where lawful and relevant;
* support a claimed lien or possessory right when separately asserted and evidenced.

A transfer may be delayed or restricted only where:

* the parties contractually agreed to a valid condition;
* a lawful lien, court order, possessory right, injunction, or other legal restriction is asserted;
* fraud, identity error, or unauthorized transfer is reasonably suspected;
* a controlled reviewer determines that proceeding would create material legal or welfare risk.

EquineSync must clearly state that it does not adjudicate the legal validity of debt, liens, or possession rights unless a separately authorized legal-review process exists.

---

# FD14 — LIENS OR POSSESSORY HOLDS

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

EquineSync shall support the recording of claimed liens, possessory holds, refusal-to-release situations, court orders, and related disputes without treating every claim as legally valid.

A lien or hold record must identify:

* the claimant;
* the claimed legal or contractual basis;
* the horse affected;
* the amount or obligation claimed, where applicable;
* the jurisdiction, if known;
* the supporting evidence;
* the date asserted;
* whether possession is currently retained;
* whether the claim is disputed;
* any expiration, release, bond, satisfaction, court action, or resolution.

The platform must distinguish:

* asserted;
* evidenced;
* disputed;
* judicially recognized;
* released;
* satisfied;
* expired;
* rejected;
* unresolved.

EquineSync must not:

* determine legal validity through AI;
* represent an asserted lien as adjudicated;
* compel physical release;
* enable self-help seizure;
* remove a restriction without appropriate authority;
* erase the history after resolution.

Horse-welfare emergencies may be escalated separately and must not be ignored because of a financial dispute.

---

# FD15 — EMERGENCY TRANSFERS

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Emergency custody, movement, evacuation, or temporary placement may occur without ordinary transfer approval when reasonably necessary to protect a horse, person, facility, or public safety.

Emergency authority may arise from:

* immediate veterinary necessity;
* fire, flood, severe weather, structural failure, or evacuation;
* abandonment;
* law-enforcement or governmental direction;
* animal-control or court authority;
* sudden incapacity, death, or disappearance of the responsible person;
* credible abuse, neglect, or safeguarding concern;
* transportation breakdown or other urgent welfare threat.

An emergency action does not automatically create permanent legal ownership.

EquineSync must distinguish:

* emergency movement;
* emergency custody;
* temporary care authority;
* legal ownership transfer;
* permanent relinquishment.

Emergency actions must be:

* limited to what is reasonably necessary;
* time-bounded where practicable;
* documented promptly;
* attributed to an accountable human actor;
* supported by evidence where available;
* notified to relevant parties when safe and lawful;
* reviewed after the immediate danger has passed.

Emergency authority must not become a backdoor method to bypass ownership disputes or ordinary consent.

# FD16 — TRANSFER REVERSAL

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

A completed transfer must not be silently deleted or overwritten.

A transfer may be reversed, rescinded, corrected, or superseded only through a controlled process.

Permissible grounds may include:

* mutual rescission;
* clerical or identity error;
* transfer to the wrong recipient;
* fraud or impersonation;
* lack of authority;
* failed condition precedent;
* court order;
* estate or fiduciary correction;
* duplicate-profile correction;
* payment or contractual reversal where legally effective;
* platform defect;
* documented return or repurchase.

The authority required to reverse a transfer must be at least as strong as the authority required to complete it, unless a court order, verified legal authority, security response, or platform-integrity correction requires otherwise.

A reversal must preserve:

* the original transfer;
* all approvals and evidence;
* the reversal reason;
* the reversing authority;
* the effective time;
* any intervening custody or care history;
* downstream corrections;
* notices;
* disputes.

EquineSync must represent the reversal as a new historical event, not as erasure of the original event.

---

# FD17 — MAXIMUM PENDING DURATION

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

An ordinary unfinished transfer may remain pending for **30 calendar days** unless a shorter or longer controlled period applies.

At minimum:

* invitations should receive reminders before expiration;
* pending transfers should be reviewed for inactivity;
* sensitive permissions must not remain unnecessarily open;
* incomplete approvals must not be treated as consent;
* expired transfers must be preserved historically but closed operationally.

A transfer may remain open longer than 30 days where:

* estate, trust, guardianship, litigation, lien, regulatory, or court processes remain active;
* the horse is subject to an ongoing welfare or possession dispute;
* the parties have documented a future closing or effective date;
* a controlled reviewer approves an extension.

Extensions should normally be granted in defined periods, such as 30, 60, or 90 days, with a stated reason and review date.

No pending transfer may remain indefinitely open without review.

---

# FD18 — INVITATION EXPIRATION

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

A standard recipient invitation expires **14 calendar days** after issuance.

The invitation may be reissued by an actor who still holds valid transfer authority.

Reissuance must:

* invalidate or supersede the prior invitation token;
* preserve the history of prior invitations;
* confirm that the recipient identity and transfer terms remain accurate;
* avoid duplicating the underlying transfer;
* restart the invitation period without resetting required approvals unless policy requires reapproval.

Higher-risk transfers may require a shorter invitation period.

Estate, organizational, fiduciary, cross-border, or legally complex transfers may use a longer controlled period.

Expiration of an invitation does not automatically cancel an otherwise valid transfer. It ends the invitation’s usability and returns the workflow to an appropriate pending or review state.

---

# FD19 — MANDATORY NOTICES

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Transfer notices must be purpose-based, role-scoped, and limited to parties with a legitimate need to know.

Depending on the transfer, mandatory notices may include:

## Before Transfer

* all verified legal owners whose approval is required;
* the intended recipient;
* authorized fiduciaries or organizational representatives;
* the current custodian or barn when operationally necessary;
* a lien claimant or restricted party where lawfully required;
* a guardian where a protected minor is involved;
* a controlled reviewer where risk thresholds are met.

## At Legal or Operational Effectiveness

* outgoing and incoming owners or authorized representatives;
* current and incoming custodians;
* parties responsible for immediate care;
* providers with active time-sensitive care responsibilities, where authorized;
* financial or billing administrators where responsibility changes;
* security or compliance reviewers where a restriction or dispute exists.

## After Completion

* all required approving parties;
* relevant custodians;
* affected billing parties;
* authorized providers;
* account administrators;
* parties whose access was revoked or materially changed, where notice is appropriate;
* audit or compliance systems.

Notices must not disclose more information than necessary.

Safeguarding, abuse, fraud, security, legal-hold, and law-enforcement circumstances may justify delayed, limited, or withheld notice.

All mandatory notices and failures must be auditable.

---

# FD20 — ESTATE, TRUST, AND FIDUCIARY EVIDENCE

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Executors, personal representatives, trustees, guardians, conservators, receivers, agents under power of attorney, corporate officers, rescue representatives, and similar actors must provide evidence sufficient to establish identity, role, scope, and current authority.

Evidence may include:

* letters testamentary;
* letters of administration;
* court appointment;
* trust certification or relevant trust excerpts;
* guardianship or conservatorship order;
* power of attorney;
* organizational resolution;
* officer certification;
* operating agreement;
* government-issued documentation;
* court order;
* verified professional attestation;
* other reliable evidence appropriate to the jurisdiction and action.

EquineSync should request the minimum necessary evidence.

Full confidential instruments should not be required when a certification, excerpt, redacted copy, or official verification is sufficient.

Evidence must be evaluated for:

* identity match;
* horse or property scope;
* effective date;
* expiration;
* revocation;
* co-fiduciary requirements;
* transaction limitations;
* jurisdiction;
* authenticity indicators.

EquineSync must not present itself as a court or provide a conclusive legal ruling. Ambiguous or contested authority must be escalated.

# FD21 — MINOR REACHING ADULTHOOD

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION AND CROSS-CANON ALIGNMENT**

When a protected minor reaches the applicable age of legal adulthood, EquineSync must initiate a controlled transition rather than automatically granting unrestricted adult authority.

The transition must evaluate:

* the user’s verified age and jurisdiction;
* current ownership interests;
* guardian-linked permissions;
* contractual capacity;
* account credentials;
* privacy consents;
* delegated authority;
* financial authority;
* horse-specific relationships;
* safeguarding restrictions;
* any court order, trust, guardianship, or conservatorship that continues beyond the age of majority.

Upon successful transition:

* the former minor may receive direct control of their own account and applicable relationships;
* guardian permissions must not automatically continue unless reauthorized or legally required;
* historical guardian actions must remain preserved;
* horse ownership must not be reassigned solely because the user reached adulthood;
* billing and contractual roles must be separately confirmed;
* safety and welfare restrictions may continue where independently justified.

The transition must follow the Master Privacy and Data Protection Model, Identity Model, Access Control Model, Agreement and Consent Model, and Relationship Model.

# FD22 — DUPLICATE CANDIDATE LINKING

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Likely duplicate horse profiles may be automatically identified and presented as duplicate candidates.

They may be automatically linked only in a nonauthoritative, reversible candidate state that does not:

* merge records;
* change ownership;
* expand permissions;
* expose cross-tenant data;
* alter authoritative history;
* transfer medical, financial, or identity information;
* represent the profiles as confirmed duplicates.

Authoritative duplicate confirmation requires appropriate human review.

Automated candidate detection must disclose:

* why the profiles appear related;
* the evidence used;
* the confidence level;
* material conflicts;
* whether the records belong to different tenants or parties;
* what actions are and are not being taken.

AI or algorithmic similarity may assist but must not make the final consequential determination.

# FD23 — POST-TRANSFER HISTORY

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Ordinary authorized users may view a limited transfer-history summary where relevant to their legitimate relationship with the horse.

The ordinary summary may include:

* transfer date;
* transfer type;
* prior and subsequent ownership or custody status where disclosure is authorized;
* source or category of authority;
* whether the transfer is completed, reversed, disputed, or corrected;
* relevant continuity notes.

Ordinary users should not automatically see:

* private contracts;
* purchase price;
* debt information;
* privileged communications;
* private addresses or contact details;
* identity-verification documents;
* internal review notes;
* safeguarding reports;
* reporter identities;
* security evidence;
* confidential estate or trust instruments;
* unrelated prior-owner information.

Access to detailed transfer history must be purpose-, role-, relationship-, and permission-scoped.

The historical chain must remain available for authorized audit, legal, evidentiary, compliance, and dispute purposes even when ordinary users receive only a limited view.

# FD25 — TEMPORARY RESTRICTIONS

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Temporary transfer restrictions may be imposed only by an authorized party or controlled process.

Potential authorities include:

* a verified legal owner acting within their rights;
* a co-owner under the applicable decision rule;
* a court, governmental authority, law-enforcement agency, or animal-control authority;
* a verified fiduciary;
* an authorized security or fraud reviewer;
* a safeguarding or welfare reviewer;
* an EquineSync platform-integrity reviewer;

Every restriction must identify:

* who imposed it;
* the authority claimed;
* the scope;
* the reason;
* the evidence;
* the start time;

* whether the restriction is disputed.

Temporary restrictions must be narrowly tailored, time-bounded where possible, and subject to review.

A platform-imposed restriction must not be represented as a judicial determination.

# FD26 — LEGACY OWNERSHIP FIELDS

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION AND CROSS-CANON ALIGNMENT**

Legacy fields such as `owner_id`, free-text owner names, imported account references, historical barn assignments, or prior database relationships do not independently establish current legal or operational authority.

They are historical assertions or migration evidence unless validated under current identity, relationship, claim, and authorization rules.

Legacy ownership fields may be used to:

* identify candidate owners;
* reconstruct history;
* support migration review;
* locate evidence;
* trigger confirmation workflows;
* preserve provenance.

They must not automatically:

* grant access;
* authorize transfer;
* override verified relationships;
* create legal ownership;
* revoke another party’s authority;
* merge horse profiles;
* satisfy fiduciary evidence requirements.

Legacy data must retain its source, date, migration status, confidence, and verification state.

# FD27 — LEGAL VS. OPERATIONAL EFFECTIVE TIME

## Decision

**ACCEPT**

Legal ownership, contractual transfer, physical possession, custody, boarding responsibility, financial responsibility, insurance responsibility, and operational control may become effective at different times.

EquineSync must model these as distinct concepts.

At minimum, a transfer may include:

* agreement date;
* legal effective date;
* payment or closing date;
* physical handoff date;
* transportation date;
* custody effective date;
* operational responsibility date;
* boarding departure date;
* boarding arrival date;
* insurance responsibility date;
* record-access transition date;
* reconciliation completion date.

The platform must not assume that physical possession proves legal ownership or that legal ownership proves current custody.

Conflicts among these dates must be visible and auditable.

# FD28 — REQUIRED COMPLETION IMPACTS

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Critical completion impacts include, as applicable:

1. required approvals have been obtained;
2. recipient identity or organizational authority has been sufficiently verified;
3. ownership or custody relationships have been updated;
4. outgoing unauthorized access has been revoked;

11. audit events have been recorded;

13. financial responsibility transitions have been recorded where applicable;
14. no unresolved critical integrity failure remains;
15. offline or external-system conflicts affecting authoritative truth have been reconciled or isolated.

Noncritical failures, such as analytics, optional integrations, marketing preferences, nonessential notifications, or secondary search indexing, may remain pending without preventing legal or operational effectiveness.

# FD29 — DUPLICATE MERGE THRESHOLDS

## Decision

**ACCEPT WITH MODIFICATION**

Horse profiles must not be merged solely because an automated system assigns a high confidence score.

The following policy applies:

## Candidate Detection

Automated systems may identify duplicate candidates using a configurable confidence model.

## Human Review

A consequential merge requires human approval.

At least one qualified reviewer is required for ordinary same-tenant merges.

Two-person approval or enhanced review is required where:

* profiles belong to different tenants;
* ownership differs;
* medical records conflict;
* financial records would combine;
* transfer history differs;
* microchip, registry, tattoo, markings, or identity evidence conflicts;
* safeguarding or legal restrictions exist;
* the merge could expose private information;
* one profile is deceased and another is active;
* confidence is below the governance-approved high-confidence threshold.

## Confidence

The exact numeric thresholds must be configurable and validated during implementation planning rather than permanently fixed in constitutional canon.

No score may override conflicting authoritative identifiers.

## Unmerge

Every merge must be reversible through a controlled unmerge process.

Unmerge must preserve:

* original profile identifiers;
* merge evidence;
* reviewer approvals;
* pre-merge provenance;
* record movement history;
* access changes;
* corrections made while merged;
* downstream effects.

A merge or unmerge must never silently rewrite historical truth.

* show history;
* accomplishments;
* tribute text;

* ownership disputes;
* private contact information;

* internal notes;
* location history that creates safety or privacy risks;
* information concerning minors without appropriate authority.

The lawful owner, authorized memorial steward, estate representative, or other verified authority may manage memorial visibility.

Where authority is disputed, memorial visibility must be restricted pending review.

Death does not erase the horse’s historical record, provenance, claims, audit trail, or relationship history.

# 44. Constitutional Summary

EquineSync shall preserve the horse while allowing the surrounding relationships to change.

The constitutional rules are:

1. A real-world horse should converge to one durable canonical identity.
2. Horse identity is separate from account, ownership, custody, possession, facility, trainer, payer, provider, and representative status.
3. Transfers append and govern history; they do not silently rewrite it.
4. Access follows current authority and approved historical purpose, not prior involvement alone.
5. Evidence remains attributed and does not become legal authority merely because it was uploaded or imported.
6. Automation and AI may assist but may not independently decide contested legal authority.
7. Emergency action protects welfare but does not create permanent ownership.
8. Financial claims and asserted liens remain separate from horse identity and require governed review.
9. Records follow classification, stewardship, consent, and permission rules rather than the horse indiscriminately.
10. Unsupported or unresolved cases must fail safely.
11. Corrections, reversals, and merges preserve lineage and audit history.
12. No implementation is authorized until the required founder decisions and implementation gates are separately approved.
