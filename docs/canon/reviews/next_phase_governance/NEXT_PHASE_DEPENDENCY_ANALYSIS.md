## Why Identity precedes alternatives

Identity defines who or what acts, which account represents which principal, how authentication evidence maps to an actor, and how attribution survives account lifecycle events. Audit requires reliable actor attribution. Agreements require party identity and capacity. Communications require recipient identity and representation. Permission evaluation consumes identity context. RF31 needs transfer-actor attribution, and proposed RF34 directly consumes the Identity canon.

Starting Audit, Agreement, Communications, or Platform Operations first would force those domains either to remain abstract or to invent identity semantics, increasing circular-authority and rework risk.

## Founder decisions required in the recommended phase

1. Disposition of candidate language that claims controlling authority before adoption.
2. Treatment of historically unavailable Version 1 and limits on preservation claims.
3. Constitutional distinction among person, account, actor, principal, representative, organization, service account, session, credential, and external identity assertion.
4. Authority order among Identity, Relationship, Permission, Claims, Stewardship, and domain canons.
5. Whether the candidate should proceed through correction to controlled adoption review.

No identity implementation, provider selection, migration, or permission change belongs in this constitutional review.
