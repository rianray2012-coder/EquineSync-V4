PART II — FD-MSP02 TAXONOMY MODIFICATION

FD-MSP02 is approved only as modified below.

EquineSync shall not use one undifferentiated Protected Participant status to combine:

- minor or age status;
- guardianship or supported-decision-making status;
- capacity for a particular decision;
- safeguarding case participation;
- reporter or witness confidentiality;
- temporary restrictions;
- allegations;
- protective controls.

The constitutional model shall separately represent:

1. Minor Status;
2. Adult Status;
3. Supported or Assisted Decision-Making Status;
4. Guardian, Custodian, Authorized Representative, and Support-Person Relationships;
5. Affected Participant;
6. Reporting Person;
7. Confidential Reporter;
8. Witness;
9. Protected Witness;
10. Responding Person;
11. Protective Restriction;
12. Prohibited-Contact Restriction;
13. Safety Plan;
14. Emergency Safeguarding Hold;
15. Minor Capability Tier;
16. Adult Eligibility State.

Protected Participant may remain a descriptive umbrella term, but it shall not independently:

- establish lack of capacity;
- remove autonomy;
- grant or deny permission;
- expose case involvement;
- imply victim status;
- imply wrongdoing;
- create guardian authority;
- create a permanent restriction.

Authorization and projection consequences must be derived from the precise evidence-supported underlying status, relationship, purpose, restriction, and controlling Permission rule.

Diagnosis, disability, communication style, age alone, vulnerability, reputation, allegation, reporter status, witness status, or convenience shall not independently establish incapacity or broad restriction.

PART III — FD-MSP06 ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATION MODIFICATION

FD-MSP06 is approved only as modified below.

A. Private one-to-one messaging

EquineSync shall prohibit private, ordinary, conversational electronic communication between a minor and a trainer, coach, instructor, staff member, volunteer, provider, transporter, photographer, customer representative, or other unrelated adult third party.

The prohibition applies regardless of whether the adult or minor initiates the communication.

B. Individualized communications

An individualized conversational communication between an adult third party and a minor must ordinarily include a currently verified guardian from the beginning and throughout the thread.

C. Controlled organizational alternative

Where guardian inclusion is:

- unavailable;
- unsafe;
- legally restricted;
- subject to a guardian conflict;
- inconsistent with the minor’s documented lawful status;
- incompatible with a protected reporting process;
- or not reasonably appropriate for a governed organizational group communication,

the communication may proceed only through an approved, logged, open-and-transparent organizational channel that includes at least one separately authorized safeguarding or supervisory adult in addition to the communicating adult.

The additional adult must not be:

- a bot;
- a generic unattended account;
- the subject of the safeguarding concern;
- an adult whose conflict makes oversight ineffective;
- or a person added only after the communication occurred.

D. Thread integrity

The system must prevent or detect:

- private branches;
- guardian or oversight-adult removal;
- delayed addition of the required oversight participant;
- disappearing communications;
- hidden edits or deletions;
- attachment or media bypass;
- comments, task annotations, or calendar replies used as hidden messaging;
- integrated email or SMS bypass;
- solicitation of secret off-platform communication;
- external-channel evasion where technically enforceable.

E. Guardian delivery failure

Where guardian inclusion is required, an expired, restricted, disabled, unreachable, or undeliverable guardian does not satisfy the rule.

The platform must not silently continue the thread as though guardian participation remains effective.

It may:

- suspend further conversational delivery;
- require corrected guardian information;
- route the communication to a controlled organizational channel;
- or permit only a qualifying emergency or protected-intake exception.

F. Protected safeguarding intake

Protected safeguarding intake remains separate from ordinary communication.

It may restrict or withhold guardian notice when:

- the guardian is implicated;
- notice creates danger or retaliation;
- notice compromises an investigation;
- another person’s protected information would be disclosed;
- controlling law or an order restricts notice;
- or a qualified safeguarding authority documents another safety basis.

Protected intake shall not create private messaging with the accused person, ordinary trainer, ordinary coach, or unrelated third party.

G. System notices

Age-appropriate, non-conversational system notices may be delivered to a minor when:

- they do not create a private adult-minor thread;
- guardian or organizational visibility is provided where required;
- replies route to a compliant channel;
- and restricted safeguarding information is not exposed.

H. Emergency communication

Emergency communication may occur only to protect immediate human or horse safety.

It must be:

- narrowly scoped;
- time-limited;
- attributable;
- preserved;
- escalated;
- and reviewed promptly.

Emergency communication does not create continuing permission for private communication.

PART IV — MEDIA, LOCATION, SEARCH, AND RECOMMENDATION DEFAULTS

I approve the following constitutional defaults:

1. Minor identity, profile, participation, media, schedule, and location information are private by default.
2. Minors shall not be generally discoverable through public search, directories, leaderboards, social recommendations, public profiles, or public media galleries.
3. Precise current location, routine movement, transportation route, pickup information, lodging information, and embedded geolocation metadata shall not be publicly exposed.
4. Public or marketing use of minor media requires valid rights, guardian authorization where applicable, and age-appropriate participant assent.
5. A minor’s protected refusal controls optional promotional media, testimonials, public profiles, optional social exposure, and optional personalization.
6. Withdrawal shall stop future authorized use where required without destroying evidence or previously lawful historical records.
7. Internal permission-scoped operational search or scheduling assistance may be allowed, but it shall not become social discovery, public recommendation, behavioral profiling, or adult targeting of minors.
8. Biometric identification, facial recognition, emotion inference, grooming scores, credibility scores, or predictive safeguarding profiles remain prohibited unless separately reconsidered through founder-reserved constitutional review.

PART V — MANDATED-REPORTING BOUNDARY

I approve the following constitutional boundary:

1. EquineSync may support intake, preservation, jurisdiction configuration, escalation, reminders, routing, and documentation related to external reporting duties.
2. EquineSync does not independently determine whether a person is legally a mandated reporter or whether a particular fact pattern legally requires a report.
3. A qualified human remains responsible for the applicable legal or professional determination.
4. An internal EquineSync or customer review must never delay, replace, narrow, or falsely represent satisfaction of an applicable external reporting duty.
5. Emergency and external reporting pathways must remain available when an internal actor, guardian, customer administrator, or ordinary reporting chain is implicated.
6. Reporting evidence must distinguish:
   - concern received;
   - internal escalation;
   - external report prepared;
   - external report transmitted;
   - transmission acknowledged;
   - follow-up requested;
   - and final disposition where known.
7. EquineSync shall not represent a platform workflow as legal advice or as proof that every applicable reporting duty has been satisfied.
8. Jurisdiction-specific reporting matrices and current-law review remain subordinate implementation and legal-readiness artifacts.

PART VI — SAFEGUARDING RECORD ACCESS, SEALING, AND UNSEALING

I approve the following constitutional rules:

1. Safeguarding records require role-, case-, subject-, purpose-, and need-to-know access.
2. Guardian status, customer ownership, payer status, ordinary administrative status, or general platform-support status does not create unrestricted access.
3. A record may be sealed to restrict ordinary visibility while preserving:
   - provenance;
   - evidentiary integrity;
   - legal or safeguarding hold;
   - audit access;
   - qualified review;
   - and required external reporting.
4. Sealing is not deletion and shall not erase the existence, authorship, or chain of custody of a record.
5. Unsealing requires:
   - qualified authority;
   - documented purpose and legal or safety basis;
   - scope;
   - affected persons;
   - time limitation where appropriate;
   - minimum-necessary projection;
   - audit evidence;
   - and post-access review.
6. Emergency break-glass access must be narrow, attributable, temporary, and reviewed.
7. Reporter and witness identities must not be disclosed through ordinary challenge or appeal merely because another person is affected by a restriction.
8. The model must distinguish:
   - allegation;
   - observation;
   - evidence;
   - temporary restriction;
   - factual finding;
   - eligibility decision;
   - external determination;
   - and legal outcome.

PART VII — CREDENTIAL, TRAINING, AND BACKGROUND-CHECK CLAIMS

I approve the following claim boundaries:

1. EquineSync shall not represent a person as “safe,” “approved,” “certified,” “cleared,” or free from risk merely because a training, background check, database search, credential, or acknowledgment exists.
2. Credential and screening states must preserve:
   - source;
   - scope;
   - date;
   - jurisdiction;
   - expiration;
   - verification state;
   - limitation;
   - dispute;
   - suspension;
   - and last review.
3. Approved evidence states may include:
   - self-asserted;
   - submitted;
   - source-verified;
   - current;
   - expired;
   - suspended;
   - disputed;
   - revoked;
   - and unable-to-verify.
4. A background check is a time-bound screening event, not a continuing safety guarantee.
5. Training completion is not professional licensure, organization approval, SafeSport status, or proof of future conduct.
6. External credentials and screening providers do not create EquineSync authority.
7. Public or customer-facing claims require exact evidence, accurate scope, current status, and separately authorized claim language.

PART VIII — MINOR CAPABILITY TIERS

I approve a governed capability-tier model rather than one undifferentiated minor account.

The tiers shall be capability and risk classes, with jurisdiction-aware age mapping handled separately.

At minimum, the model must distinguish:

A. Guardian-Managed Participation

- guardian manages material permissions;
- limited participant visibility and age-appropriate interaction;
- no independent agreements, payments, transport authority, medical authority, safeguarding authority, ownership authority, or private adult messaging.

B. Supervised Participant

- may view assigned schedules and permitted horse information;
- may acknowledge or complete low-risk, supervised tasks;
- may submit concerns and exercise age-appropriate assent or refusal;
- may not exercise high-risk, medical, financial, ownership, custody, transport, or unrestricted record authority.

C. Advanced Supervised Participant

- may receive broader self-service and operational capability when supported by age, competence, relationship, purpose, and guardian or lawful authority;
- remains subject to communication, media, health, financial, transport, and safeguarding limits;
- receives no automatic adult authority.

D. Transition-to-Adult Review

- applies when majority or another material legal-status transition occurs;
- requires recalculation of guardian-derived authority;
- invites the participant to control their own account;
- reviews consents, privacy, communication, and payment relationships;
- preserves historical evidence;
- and continues restrictions only where independently supported.

No tier may independently authorize:

- medication or clinical decisions;
- emergency treatment disposition;
- horse ownership or transfer;
- financial liability;
- contract execution beyond lawful capacity;
- unsupervised high-risk horse care;
- pickup or transport of another person;
- safeguarding investigation;
- access to unrelated confidential records;
- or private adult-minor messaging.

PART IX — COMMUNICATION OBSERVABILITY

I approve the following constitutional observability model:

1. Required metadata may include:
   - sender;
   - recipients;
   - required guardian or oversight participant;
   - thread membership changes;
   - timestamps;
   - delivery status;
   - attachment presence;
   - edits;
   - deletions;
   - reply and branch relationships;
   - integration channel;
   - and audit correlation.
2. EquineSync shall not create routine unrestricted surveillance of minor communications.
3. Communication content may be accessed only under a defined role, purpose, legal or safeguarding basis, case scope, minimum-necessary projection, and audit trail.
4. Protected safeguarding intake must use more restrictive content access than ordinary communications.
5. Automated systems may surface urgent language or missing-information signals for authorized human review but may not determine credibility, abuse, guilt, danger, discipline, guardian fitness, or reporting duty.
6. Content access, support access, export, bulk access, and administrative override must be separately logged and reviewable.
7. Guardian visibility shall not expose protected intake, another minor’s information, or information withheld for a documented safety or legal reason.

PART X — SAFESPORT AND EXTERNAL-FRAMEWORK CLAIMS

I approve the following claim limits:

1. EquineSync shall not claim:
   - SafeSport certification;
   - SafeSport approval;
   - universal SafeSport compliance;
   - legal compliance;
   - background-check compliance;
   - or safeguarding certification
   without an exact, current, evidence-supported basis and separate founder approval.
2. EquineSync may accurately describe specific product controls without claiming external certification.
3. Any SafeSport or comparable framework claim must identify:
   - the applicable framework and version;
   - the organization and activity within scope;
   - jurisdiction and applicability;
   - implementation evidence;
   - exceptions and limitations;
   - review date;
   - qualified legal or compliance review where appropriate;
   - and founder approval of the exact public language.
4. EquineSync must not imply that a framework applying to one sports movement, governing body, or jurisdiction automatically applies to every barn, trainer, competition, or customer.
5. External-framework status shall not independently grant platform authority or prove that a person or organization is safe.
