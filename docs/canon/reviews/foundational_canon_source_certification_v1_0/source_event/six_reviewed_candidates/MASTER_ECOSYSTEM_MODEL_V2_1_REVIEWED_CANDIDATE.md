# Purpose and Constitutional Function

This Model defines the durable architecture of the EquineSync ecosystem: the first-class entities that may exist, the relationships that connect them, the sources of authority that govern them, and the rules by which records, work, money, communication, analytics, automation, and external systems interact.

It is the bridge between the Product Vision and specialized constitutional models. It does not prescribe tables, routes, screens, or vendor choices. It establishes the conceptual skeleton that prevents the platform from becoming a collection of disconnected modules.

> One ecosystem, many contexts, one governed truth model.

# Ecosystem Thesis

EquineSync is a horse-centered, relationship-aware, permission-governed operating ecosystem. The horse is the enduring center of continuity, while people, organizations, businesses, facilities, providers, and platform actors retain their own independent identities and lifecycles.

Modules are projections over shared constitutional truth. They may organize work differently for different users, but they may not invent competing definitions of horse, person, business, facility, relationship, authority, record, event, or obligation.

# Separation and Non-Collapse Rules

- A person is not an account; an account is not an actor; an actor is not authority.
- A role label is not a governed relationship, and a relationship is not automatically a permission.
- A business is not a facility; a facility is not a legal entity; a brand is not a merchant account.
- Legal ownership, custody, possession, boarding, training, care authority, and payment responsibility remain distinct.
- A payer is not necessarily the owner, guardian, customer, service recipient, or contracting party.
- A signature proves a signing event, not the signer’s authority, the agreement’s validity, or performance.
- A vendor record, imported record, AI output, report, cache, search result, or export is not automatically canonical truth.
- Authorship, stewardship, access, retention, portability, transferability, and legal control of a record are separate concepts.
- Public discoverability does not create private access, and marketplace participation does not create a service relationship.
- Administrative capability does not create a business or legal right to inspect customer information.

# Relationship and Authority Architecture

Material connections must be explicit, scoped, temporal, attributable, reviewable, and revocable where the underlying relationship permits. Relationship truth supplies evidence to authorization, but access must still satisfy purpose, permission, consent, safeguarding, sensitivity, and restriction controls.

## Authority Resolution Order

1. Identify the actor and authenticated account or service identity.
2. Identify the relevant horse, person, organization, business, facility, record, task, or transaction.
3. Resolve the governing relationship and its effective period.
4. Resolve authority claims, restrictions, disputes, consent, and delegated powers.
5. Apply permission, purpose, sensitivity, minor, safeguarding, and field-level rules.
6. Apply emergency, offline, integration, and administrative constraints.
7. Record the authorization basis and material outcome in the audit system.

No single shortcut may bypass this chain merely because a user is an owner, manager, provider, employee, administrator, or payer.

# Time, State, and Event Architecture

- Material changes must be represented as attributable events and versioned state transitions.
- Event time, effective time, recorded time, received time, verified time, corrected time, synchronized time, and processing time must remain distinguishable.
- Current state must be derivable without destroying prior valid states or obscuring corrections.
- Future-effective and retroactive changes require explicit treatment and must not silently rewrite historical projections.
- Offline, delayed, imported, and replayed events require provenance, idempotency, conflict handling, and reconciliation.
- Last-write-wins behavior is prohibited for consequential ownership, authority, care, medical, financial, safeguarding, or identity truth.

# Workflow and Domain Ownership Rule

A workflow belongs constitutionally to the domain that owns the underlying truth, even when it is initiated or displayed elsewhere. Calendar owns scheduling semantics; Relationship owns relationship truth; Permission owns access authority; Audit owns evidentiary audit requirements; Financial Truth owns monetary state; Reporting owns official analytical publication.

User-interface placement, navigation ownership, team ownership, or database location does not transfer constitutional ownership.

# Cross-Tenant, Shared-Care, and Public Boundaries

- Tenant isolation is the default and must not be weakened for convenience.
- Cross-tenant processing requires a defined legal, operational, service, delegated-care, marketplace, emergency, public, or regulatory basis.
- Shared care must be horse-specific, task-limited, purpose-limited, time-limited, attributable, revocable, and minimally disclosive.
- Community-centered barn assistance may expose only the information needed to perform the assigned care task.
- Public discovery is opt-in and may not expose minors, protected people, exact private location, private owner data, financial information, safeguarding records, or unrelated medical history.
- Cross-tenant analytics must use governed aggregation, de-identification, minimum cohort rules, and prohibited re-identification controls.

# External Systems, Devices, and Extensions

External systems may authenticate, transmit, store, sign, settle, synchronize, calculate, or process within an approved scope. They do not become constitutional authority and may not expand access beyond EquineSync’s governed rules.

- Every integration requires source-of-truth designation, directionality, conflict behavior, revocation, audit, retention, failure, and exit rules.
- Connected devices and sensors provide observations, not unquestionable truth; confidence, calibration, latency, and failure must be represented.
- Plugins and extensions must register owned data, permissions, event types, retention, audit, incident, and uninstall behavior.
- Feature flags may control exposure but may not create authority, waive consent, bypass review, or conceal a permanent governance decision.
- Provider neutrality, portability, safe degradation, and migration capability are required for material external dependencies.

# Ecosystem Change Governance

New first-class entities, relationship types, authority claims, workflow classes, data classifications, payment roles, public projections, AI actions, integrations, or extension points require constitutional placement before implementation.

## Required Change Questions

- Who or what is the canonical subject?
- Which domain owns the truth?
- Which relationships and authority claims are required?
- What data is sensitive, transferable, or non-transferable?
- What happens when the relationship ends?
- What must be audited, retained, corrected, exported, or deleted?
- How does the capability behave offline, during failure, and after vendor exit?
- Does the change create cross-tenant, minor, safeguarding, financial, medical, or welfare risk?
