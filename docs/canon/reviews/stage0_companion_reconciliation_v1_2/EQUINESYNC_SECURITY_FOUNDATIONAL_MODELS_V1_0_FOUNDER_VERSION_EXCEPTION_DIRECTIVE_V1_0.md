## 3. Reconciled Domain Boundaries

### 3.1 Encryption, Retention, and Deletion

Privacy determines whether deletion or restriction is required or permitted. Record Stewardship determines whether retention, legal hold, evidence, or historical preservation prevents disposition. The Encryption and Key Management Model executes only an authorized cryptographic action. Accidental or unauthorized loss of key material is a security and availability incident, not valid deletion.

### 3.2 Incident Response, Resilience, and Platform Operations

Security Incident Response and Platform Resilience are coupled peer authorities.

- Security Incident Response owns security-event classification, containment, investigation, breach and disclosure assessment, approved incident communications, and security-specific closure.
- Platform Resilience owns service criticality, backup, restoration, RTO/RPO, failover, failback, safe degradation, reconciliation, and recovery evidence.
- Platform Operations executes approved containment, service restoration, deployment rollback, failover, and return-to-service actions. It does not independently redefine incident, disclosure, backup, restoration, or continuity doctrine.

For current constitutional interpretation, the historical phrase identifying Platform Operations as the “owning canon” of the Resilience Operational Model is treated as an execution and operational-accountability relationship, not a transfer of resilience subject-matter ownership.

### 3.3 Privacy and Notice

Privacy owns privacy-impact analysis, rights, processing obligations, and privacy-specific notification requirements. Incident Response owns the approved incident facts, affected population, disclosure decision, and timing authority. Communication, Notification, and Notice owns channel execution, delivery states, accessibility, retries, and delivery evidence.
