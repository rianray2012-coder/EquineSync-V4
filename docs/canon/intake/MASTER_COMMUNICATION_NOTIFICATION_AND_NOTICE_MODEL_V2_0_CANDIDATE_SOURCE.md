# MASTER COMMUNICATION, NOTIFICATION, AND NOTICE MODEL

**Document Class:** Constitutional Canon  
**Canonical Name:** Master Communication, Notification, and Notice Model  
**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Expanded Constitutional Candidate for Controlled Founder Review  
**Authority Level:** Platform-Wide Master Governance Model  
**Applies To:** All EquineSync domains, actors, organizations, facilities, horses, records, workflows, agreements, billing activities, emergencies, integrations, and communication surfaces  
**Implementation Authorization:** Not granted. This document governs design and review only until introduced through an authorized RF or equivalent implementation gate.  
**Supersession Rule:** This model supersedes inconsistent lower-order communication, notification, reminder, alert, messaging, and notice rules. A later document may override it only if the override is founder-approved, express, narrowly stated, dependency-reviewed, versioned, and entered into the Canon Index

## 1. PURPOSE

This Master Communication, Notification, and Notice Model establishes the constitutional rules governing how EquineSync creates, classifies, authorizes, routes, delivers, records, escalates, acknowledges, preserves, and proves communications across the platform.

The model is designed to ensure that EquineSync communications are:

- directed to the correct person or organization;
- sent through an authorized and appropriate channel;
- understandable, timely, and proportionate to the underlying event;
- distinguishable as informational, operational, contractual, financial, safety-related, regulatory, or legal;
- supported by reliable delivery, acknowledgment, and audit evidence;
- respectful of user preferences without allowing preferences to defeat legal, safety, security, or contractual duties;
- resilient across organizational boundaries, ownership transfers, staffing changes, horse transfers, facility changes, and account lifecycle events;
- compliant with applicable consent, privacy, electronic communications, records, accessibility, and notice requirements; and
- capable of supporting future adapters, automation, artificial intelligence, and third-party delivery services without weakening human accountability.

This document governs the communication lifecycle. It does not independently authorize a specific message, legal notice, emergency alert, marketing campaign, integration, automation, production mutation, or external delivery service.


### 1.1 Constitutional Objective

The constitutional objective of this model is to prevent five recurring platform failures:

1. a message being sent by a person who lacks authority;
2. a communication reaching the wrong person, role, organization, or contact capacity;
3. a low-assurance channel being treated as sufficient for a high-consequence notice;
4. technical transmission being misrepresented as human receipt, acknowledgment, or legal effectiveness; and
5. communication history being altered, lost, fragmented, or stripped of the evidence needed to understand what occurred.

### 1.2 Governing Distinction

EquineSync must preserve the distinction among:

- **conversation**, which supports human exchange;
- **notification**, which creates awareness;
- **alert**, which demands heightened attention;
- **reminder**, which repeats an existing obligation or event;
- **request**, which seeks a response or action;
- **instruction**, which directs conduct within an authorized relationship;
- **authorization request**, which seeks permission;
- **agreement communication**, which presents or records assent-bearing terms;
- **formal notice**, which may affect legal, contractual, financial, privacy, security, safety, or access rights; and
- **evidence communication**, which preserves or transmits a record of what occurred.

These categories may share infrastructure, but they may not be collapsed into one undifferentiated “message” object.

### 1.3 Constitutional Outcomes

A compliant communication system must be capable of answering, without speculation:

- who caused the communication to be created;
- on whose authority it was issued;
- which canonical event or record triggered it;
- who was selected to receive it and in what capacity;
- what content and attachments were actually issued;
- which channel or sequence was used;
- what each channel provider reported;
- whether the content was displayed, opened, acknowledged, accepted, disputed, or acted upon;
- whether delivery failed and what happened next;
- whether the communication was corrected, superseded, withdrawn, or placed under legal hold; and
- which version of policy, template, agreement, preference, consent, and relationship state governed the event.

## 2. CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLES

### 2.1 Communication Is a Governed Platform Action

A communication is not merely text presented to a user. It is a governed platform action that may alter awareness, expectations, duties, deadlines, safety decisions, legal rights, payment obligations, operational behavior, or evidentiary posture.

Every material communication must therefore have:

1. an identifiable source;
2. an authorized sender or issuing authority;
3. a defined recipient population;
4. a lawful and permitted purpose;
5. an appropriate classification;
6. an approved channel or channel sequence;
7. an issue time;
8. a content version;
9. a retention treatment;
10. a traceable delivery state; and
11. where required, an acknowledgment, acceptance, response, or completion state.

### 2.2 Meaning Governs Over Label

A communication is governed by its substantive effect, not merely by the name assigned to it.

For example:

- a message labeled “reminder” that threatens suspension is a consequential account notice;
- a push alert requiring immediate medication action is a safety-critical operational alert;
- an in-app banner changing payment terms is a contractual or financial notice;
- an email announcing a new optional feature is an informational communication;
- a text confirming a lesson time may be an operational notification;
- a request for signature is an agreement execution communication.

EquineSync must not use softer labels to avoid stronger governance requirements.

### 2.3 Correct Recipient Before Convenient Recipient

The platform must route communications to the person or entity who possesses the relevant authority, duty, interest, relationship, or legal status, even when another recipient would be easier to reach.

### 2.4 Severity Determines Delivery Strength

The more serious the consequence of nonreceipt, delay, misunderstanding, or inaction, the stronger the required communication controls must be.

### 2.5 Preferences Do Not Defeat Mandatory Notice

User preferences may shape ordinary communications, but they cannot disable communications required for:

- emergencies;
- horse welfare;
- human safety;
- account security;
- legal or regulatory compliance;
- contractual performance;
- payment default, suspension, or termination;
- material policy changes;
- privacy rights;
- consent withdrawal;
- data breach or security incident response;
- legal hold or preservation obligations; or
- other founder-approved mandatory notice classes.

### 2.6 Delivery Is Not the Same as Receipt

EquineSync must distinguish among:

- created;
- queued;
- transmitted;
- accepted by a delivery provider;
- delivered to a destination;
- displayed;
- opened;
- read;
- acknowledged;
- accepted;
- rejected;
- acted upon;
- completed;
- failed;
- expired;
- superseded; and
- withdrawn.

No unsupported inference of actual receipt may be made merely because a provider accepted a message.

### 2.7 Communication Evidence Is a Record

Communication metadata, delivery evidence, acknowledgment events, content versions, recipient lists, preference overrides, and related audit data are governed records under the Master Record Stewardship and Retention Model.

### 2.8 Human Accountability Must Remain Visible

Automation, artificial intelligence, templates, scheduling rules, and integrations may assist communication, but must not erase the identity of the accountable human or organizational authority responsible for the communication.


### 2.9 Communication Cannot Create Authority That Does Not Otherwise Exist

Sending a communication does not create ownership, agency, permission, consent, guardianship, contractual authority, veterinary authority, payment responsibility, employment authority, or legal standing that the sender did not already possess.

### 2.10 Recipient Capacity Must Be Explicit

Where a person may receive in multiple capacities, EquineSync must identify the capacity in which the communication is delivered, including:

- personally;
- as horse owner;
- as guardian;
- as facility representative;
- as employee or contractor;
- as billing contact;
- as emergency contact;
- as legal notice contact;
- as authorized agent; or
- as service provider.

The same contact destination must not cause capacities to be silently merged.

### 2.11 Content and Transport Are Separate Governed Layers

The authoritative communication content, recipient resolution, and legal classification must remain distinct from the transport service used to transmit them. Replacing an email, SMS, push, signature, or mail vendor must not alter the meaning, authority, retention, or evidence model.

### 2.12 Material Communications Must Be Immutable After Issue

After a material communication is issued, its authoritative content and attachments must not be silently edited. Corrections, withdrawals, and superseding communications must create new governed events linked to the original.

### 2.13 Failed Communication Does Not Erase the Underlying Duty

A failed delivery may trigger escalation or affect legal sufficiency, but it does not automatically erase the underlying task, emergency, contractual obligation, payment obligation, or safety condition. The platform must separately model the underlying duty and the attempt to communicate it.

### 2.14 No Artificial Certainty

EquineSync must represent communication evidence according to what is actually known. The platform must not convert provider events, pixels, client-side telemetry, or probabilistic signals into unsupported claims that a human read, understood, agreed to, or acted upon a communication.

## 4. DEFINITIONS

### 4.1 Communication

Any transmitted, displayed, generated, recorded, or delivered expression intended to inform, request, instruct, warn, remind, confirm, document, solicit, authorize, bind, escalate, or preserve awareness.

### 4.2 Notification

A communication intended to make a recipient aware of an event, change, status, task, deadline, or condition.

### 4.3 Notice

A communication that may affect rights, duties, consent, legal position, contractual status, payment status, privacy rights, service access, safety obligations, or another consequential interest.

### 4.4 Alert

A time-sensitive communication requiring heightened attention because delay may create operational, safety, welfare, security, financial, or legal harm.

### 4.5 Reminder

A supplemental communication referring to a previously established duty, event, appointment, task, or deadline.

### 4.6 Message

A person-to-person, person-to-group, organization-to-person, or system-assisted communication that may be conversational or transactional.

### 4.7 Delivery Channel

The medium through which a communication is transmitted or displayed.

### 4.8 Recipient

A person, role, organization, account, group, guardian, authorized agent, or endpoint intended to receive a communication.

### 4.9 Authorized Recipient

A recipient who has a valid relationship, permission, authority, duty, or legal basis to receive the communication.

### 4.10 Issuing Authority

The human, organization, or governed system process authorized to cause the communication to be issued.

### 4.11 Sender Identity

The identity displayed to the recipient as the source of the communication.

### 4.12 Communication Event

A discrete lifecycle event associated with creation, routing, transmission, delivery, display, acknowledgment, action, failure, escalation, withdrawal, or retention.

### 4.13 Mandatory Communication

A communication that cannot be disabled because of legal, contractual, safety, security, welfare, privacy, or platform-governance requirements.

### 4.14 Preference-Eligible Communication

A communication whose channel, frequency, batching, timing, or category may be adjusted by the recipient.

### 4.15 Acknowledgment

A recorded indication that the recipient affirmatively confirmed awareness.

### 4.16 Acceptance

A recorded indication that the recipient affirmatively agreed to a proposed term, request, obligation, or action.

### 4.17 Read State

A technical state indicating that content was displayed or opened. A read state is not automatically equivalent to acknowledgment or legal receipt.

### 4.18 Escalation

A governed increase in urgency, recipient scope, channel strength, supervisory visibility, or operational intervention following defined conditions.

### 4.19 Recipient Capacity

The legal, operational, organizational, relational, or representative role in which a person or entity receives a communication.

### 4.20 Authoritative Content

The exact content, attachments, incorporated documents, metadata, and action controls that constitute the issued communication.

### 4.21 Delivery Attempt

A discrete effort to transmit a communication through one channel to one destination.

### 4.22 Delivery Evidence

Provider, device, application, or human-generated evidence concerning transmission, delivery, display, access, acknowledgment, acceptance, refusal, failure, or action.

### 4.23 Consequential Communication

A communication for which nonreceipt, misunderstanding, delay, or unauthorized disclosure may materially affect safety, welfare, money, access, privacy, security, rights, duties, or legal position.

### 4.24 Communication Policy Snapshot

The preserved set of rules, preferences, consents, relationships, permissions, contact destinations, template versions, and escalation logic used when a communication was resolved and issued.

### 4.25 Service Address or Notice Destination

A contact destination expressly designated by law, contract, policy, or verified user action for a defined class of formal notice.

### 4.26 Manual Communication Event

A phone call, in-person statement, hand delivery, postal delivery, or external communication performed outside the automated transport layer but recorded in EquineSync as governed evidence.

### 4.27 Communication Package

The content, metadata, recipient logic, channel rules, delivery evidence, attachments, language, accessibility treatment, and retention instructions associated with a communication.

## 4A. COMMUNICATION OBJECT AND THREAD MODEL

### 4A.1 Object Separation

The canonical communication architecture must distinguish at least:

- communication intent;
- communication package;
- recipient resolution;
- recipient instance;
- channel plan;
- delivery attempt;
- delivery event;
- response event;
- escalation event;
- correction or supersession event;
- evidence package; and
- retained thread or case context.

A single announcement to fifty recipients is one communication intent but may create fifty recipient instances and multiple delivery attempts per recipient.

### 4A.2 Threading Rules

Threading must not obscure independent obligations. A new deadline, new agreement version, new invoice, new emergency episode, or new legal notice must have its own canonical identity even when displayed inside an existing conversation.

### 4A.3 Canonical Source Linkage

Every consequential communication must link to the authoritative domain object that gave rise to it, such as:

- horse record;
- incident;
- medication order;
- task;
- calendar event;
- invoice;
- payment attempt;
- agreement;
- consent request;
- account action;
- privacy request;
- security event;
- facility closure;
- transfer event; or
- legal hold.

The communication must not become the sole source of truth for the underlying event.

### 4A.4 Derived Display

Notification centers, inboxes, dashboards, badges, digests, and timelines are derived views. Deleting or hiding a derived display must not destroy the authoritative communication record where retention applies.

## 4B. COMMUNICATION LIFECYCLE AND STATE MACHINE

### 4B.1 Core Lifecycle

A material communication should move through governed states such as:

1. `DRAFTED`;
2. `VALIDATED`;
3. `PENDING_APPROVAL`;
4. `APPROVED`;
5. `SCHEDULED` or `READY_TO_ISSUE`;
6. `RECIPIENTS_RESOLVED`;
7. `ISSUED`;
8. `DELIVERY_IN_PROGRESS`;
9. `DELIVERED`, `PARTIALLY_DELIVERED`, or `DELIVERY_FAILED`;
10. `DISPLAYED` or `OPENED`, when supportable;
11. `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `DISPUTED`, `ACTED_UPON`, or `NO_RESPONSE`;
12. `ESCALATED`, where required;
13. `RESOLVED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `WITHDRAWN`, or `CLOSED`; and
14. `RETAINED`, `HELD`, or `DISPOSED` under record governance.

### 4B.2 State Independence

Recipient state and communication state must remain distinct. One recipient may acknowledge while another fails delivery. Aggregate state must not erase recipient-level evidence.

### 4B.3 Transition Authority

Each state transition must identify whether it was caused by:

- a human actor;
- an organizational role;
- a domain event;
- a scheduled rule;
- a provider callback;
- an integration;
- a support intervention; or
- an authorized automated process.

### 4B.4 Terminal and Nonterminal Failures

A channel failure is not necessarily a communication failure if an authorized fallback succeeds. A communication must remain nonterminal while required escalation or fallback paths remain open.

### 4B.5 Idempotency

Retries, webhook replays, device reconnections, and job restarts must not create duplicate notices, duplicate assent, duplicate escalation, or conflicting evidence.

## 4C. COMMUNICATION ASSURANCE LEVELS

EquineSync must assign an assurance level separate from subject classification.

### 4C.1 A0: Informal Assurance

Suitable for low-risk conversation and optional information. No identity or delivery proof beyond ordinary authenticated use is required.

### 4C.2 A1: Attributable Assurance

Requires a known sender, known recipient account or destination, issued content, and basic transmission evidence.

### 4C.3 A2: Verified Delivery Assurance

Requires verified or trusted contact information, preserved content, delivery evidence, failure handling, and recipient-level status.

### 4C.4 A3: Acknowledged Assurance

Requires affirmative acknowledgment by an authenticated or otherwise verified recipient.

### 4C.5 A4: Assent or Authorization Assurance

Requires identity, authority, content version, affirmative action, intent to accept or authorize, and durable evidence governed by the Master Agreement, Consent, and Authorization Model.

### 4C.6 A5: Formal or External Proof Assurance

Requires a contractually or legally appropriate method, which may include electronic signature evidence, tracked delivery, certified mail, courier, process service, notarization, witness evidence, or another jurisdiction-specific method.

### 4C.7 Assurance Selection

The communication class establishes the minimum assurance level, but law, contract, jurisdiction, risk, user vulnerability, or founder policy may require a higher level.

## 5. COMMUNICATION CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK

Every material communication must be assigned a primary class and may also receive one or more secondary tags.

### 5.1 Class C0: Conversational

Examples:

- ordinary direct messages;
- informal coordination;
- nonbinding questions;
- social or community communication.

Governance characteristics:

- lowest formality;
- user-controlled channel;
- ordinary retention;
- no presumption of legal notice;
- may not be used to bypass mandatory notice workflows.

### 5.2 Class C1: Informational

Examples:

- feature announcements;
- general facility updates;
- educational content;
- nonurgent status summaries;
- optional newsletters.

Governance characteristics:

- preference eligible;
- may be batched;
- opt-out permitted where lawful;
- no acknowledgment normally required.

### 5.3 Class C2: Operational

Examples:

- lesson changes;
- task assignments;
- feed changes;
- turnout instructions;
- service appointments;
- arena scheduling updates;
- workflow status changes.

Governance characteristics:

- routed to operationally responsible actors;
- may require read or acknowledgment state;
- timing based on the operational event;
- may escalate if the task is unaccepted or overdue.

### 5.4 Class C3: Time-Sensitive Operational

Examples:

- same-day schedule changes;
- missed medication task;
- late farrier or veterinarian arrival;
- facility closure;
- horse transport delay;
- staffing coverage failure.

Governance characteristics:

- stronger channel rules;
- defined response window;
- escalation permitted;
- fallback channel required where material.

### 5.5 Class C4: Safety, Welfare, or Emergency

Examples:

- horse injury;
- missing horse;
- suspected colic;
- medication risk;
- fire;
- severe weather;
- human injury;
- biosecurity event;
- facility evacuation;
- urgent veterinary authorization request.

Governance characteristics:

- mandatory;
- highest-priority routing;
- multi-channel delivery where available;
- acknowledgment or direct response required;
- escalation to alternates and supervisors;
- suppression preferences do not apply;
- durable audit record required.

### 5.6 Class C5: Financial

Examples:

- invoice issuance;
- payment due reminder;
- failed payment;
- disputed charge;
- deposit requirement;
- service credit;
- payment plan;
- account delinquency.

Governance characteristics:

- content must identify amount, basis, due date, payment method, and dispute path where applicable;
- delivery evidence retained;
- reminders distinguished from default notices;
- legal or contractual consequences require stronger notice controls.

### 5.7 Class C6: Contractual, Consent, or Authorization

Examples:

- boarding contract;
- training agreement;
- lesson agreement;
- participation waiver;
- emergency medical authorization;
- photo release;
- consent request;
- amendment notice;
- renewal notice;
- cancellation confirmation.

Governance characteristics:

- must identify parties and document version;
- governed by the Master Agreement, Consent, and Authorization Model;
- acceptance evidence required where binding;
- no silent consent unless expressly lawful and canon-authorized;
- material changes require clear notice.

### 5.8 Class C7: Account, Access, Privacy, or Security

Examples:

- sign-in alert;
- password reset;
- account recovery;
- permission change;
- suspicious activity;
- data export readiness;
- privacy request status;
- service suspension;
- account termination.

Governance characteristics:

- mandatory where security or legal rights are implicated;
- identity verification may be required;
- sensitive details minimized;
- delivery to trusted destinations;
- escalation and fraud controls apply.

### 5.9 Class C8: Legal, Regulatory, or Formal Notice

Examples:

- legal hold notice;
- subpoena-related preservation;
- regulatory disclosure;
- terms amendment;
- privacy policy change;
- breach notification;
- termination for cause;
- cure notice;
- formal demand;
- legal service support record.

Governance characteristics:

- highest evidentiary controls;
- approved template and authority required;
- exact content version preserved;
- delivery method selected according to applicable law or contract;
- legal review may be required;
- may require certified, tracked, or external delivery outside EquineSync.

### 5.10 Class C9: Marketing or Promotional

Examples:

- offers;
- referral campaigns;
- promotional messages;
- upsell communications;
- sponsored content.

Governance characteristics:

- affirmative legal basis required;
- opt-out and preference controls required;
- must not be disguised as operational or safety communication;
- separate from mandatory notices;
- frequency controls apply.

## 7. SOURCE AND AUTHORITY MODEL

### 7.1 Permitted Sources

A communication may originate from:

1. a verified person;
2. an authorized organizational role;
3. a governed workflow;
4. a system event;
5. a scheduled rule;
6. an approved integration;
7. a support or administrative operator acting within scope;
8. an authorized delegate;
9. an approved template; or
10. a future AI capability expressly authorized by separate canon.

### 7.2 Authority Must Be Contextual

Authority to communicate depends on the relationship and subject matter.

Examples:

- a horse owner may authorize communications concerning that horse but not unrelated facility financial matters;
- a trainer may issue training instructions but may not amend a boarding agreement without authority;
- a barn manager may issue facility closure notices;
- a service provider may send appointment updates;
- a guardian may receive communications for a minor;
- a staff member may communicate task status but may not issue a legal termination notice;
- an organization may issue payment notices through authorized billing roles.

### 7.3 Delegated Communication Authority

Delegation must be:

- explicit;
- role-based or record-specific;
- time-bounded where appropriate;
- revocable;
- auditable;
- limited to defined communication classes; and
- incapable of expanding the delegate’s underlying access rights.

### 7.4 Impersonation Prohibited

The platform must not allow a user, organization, integration, or automated process to appear as another person or authority without clear disclosure of agency or delegated status.

### 7.5 System-Generated Communications

System-generated communications must identify:

- the governing event;
- the organization or context on whose behalf the communication is sent;
- whether the message was automated;
- the responsible authority where material; and
- the appropriate response or escalation path.

## 7A. APPROVAL AND FOUR-EYES CONTROLS

### 7A.1 Approval Requirement

Communications requiring approval must not be issued merely because the drafter has permission to create them.

### 7A.2 Approval Classes

At minimum, the following should be evaluated for two-person or specialized approval:

- termination, suspension, default, or cure notices;
- material agreement amendments;
- data breach or regulatory notices;
- mass emergency communications;
- legal hold instructions;
- disputed ownership or abandonment notices;
- disciplinary notices;
- communications to a large cross-organizational population;
- public statements concerning an incident;
- communications generated from sensitive analytics; and
- high-risk AI-assisted drafts.

### 7A.3 Separation of Duties

Where proportionate, the system should distinguish:

- drafter;
- factual reviewer;
- legal or policy reviewer;
- issuing authority;
- delivery operator; and
- evidence custodian.

### 7A.4 Emergency Exception

Emergency authority may permit immediate issue without ordinary preapproval, but the exception must be narrow, logged, and subject to prompt after-action review.

## 8. RECIPIENT MODEL

### 8.1 Recipient Selection

Recipient selection must be based on one or more of the following:

- direct legal interest;
- contractual status;
- ownership;
- guardianship;
- operational responsibility;
- assigned task;
- emergency authority;
- facility role;
- professional role;
- consent;
- account ownership;
- privacy right;
- payment responsibility;
- service relationship;
- delegation;
- statutory entitlement; or
- founder-approved platform rule.

### 8.2 Individual and Organizational Recipients

EquineSync must distinguish:

- a person receiving personally;
- a person receiving as an organizational representative;
- a role-based destination;
- a shared organizational inbox;
- a guardian receiving for a minor;
- an agent receiving for a principal;
- an emergency contact;
- a billing contact;
- a legal notice contact;
- an operational contact;
- a technical administrator; and
- an inactive or former relationship holder.

### 8.3 Multi-Recipient Communications

The platform must control whether recipients are:

- visible to each other;
- hidden from each other;
- grouped by organization;
- grouped by relationship;
- individually addressed;
- copied for awareness;
- required to acknowledge independently; or
- included only after escalation.

### 8.4 Least-Disclosure Rule

A communication must disclose only the information reasonably necessary for that recipient’s role, purpose, and authority.

### 8.5 Former, Suspended, or Transferred Relationships

Communications following a transfer, termination, suspension, or role change must follow the Master Identity, Account, and Actor Model and the Master Relationship Model.

Historical recipients do not retain continuing entitlement merely because they previously had access.

### 8.6 Minors and Guardians

Communications involving minors must:

- identify whether the minor, guardian, or both may receive the communication;
- follow age, consent, Safe Sport, and platform policy requirements;
- avoid exposing sensitive information beyond authorized recipients;
- preserve guardian authority where required;
- support direct minor communications only where permitted;
- escalate safety matters appropriately.

## 8A. RECIPIENT RESOLUTION AND CONTACT DESTINATION GOVERNANCE

### 8A.1 Resolution Inputs

Recipient resolution may use only governed inputs, including:

- active relationship state;
- role assignment;
- horse ownership or custody;
- guardian status;
- delegated authority;
- billing responsibility;
- agreement party status;
- emergency contact priority;
- legal notice designation;
- jurisdiction;
- communication preference;
- consent state;
- account status; and
- applicable suppression or override rule.

### 8A.2 Resolution Snapshot

For consequential communications, EquineSync must preserve the recipient-resolution snapshot used at issue time. Later changes to relationships, contacts, or roles must not rewrite historical recipient logic.

### 8A.3 Contact Destination Types

Contact destinations must be typed, not merely stored as generic strings. Supported types may include:

- verified personal email;
- verified business email;
- mobile number;
- emergency number;
- legal notice address;
- billing address;
- postal service address;
- guardian destination;
- organizational inbox;
- integration endpoint;
- device token.

### 8A.4 Destination Confidence

The platform should record whether a destination is:

- unverified;
- user-provided;
- verified;
- organization-verified;
- contract-designated;
- legally designated;
- stale;
- disputed;
- revoked;
- invalid.

### 8A.5 Ambiguity Handling

When two plausible recipients or destinations conflict, the system must not guess for a consequential communication. It must apply an approved precedence rule or require human resolution.

- future approved channels.

### 9.2 Channel Suitability

Channel selection must consider:

- communication class;
- urgency;
- legal sufficiency;
- recipient preference;
- accessibility;
- deliverability;
- recipient verification;
- data sensitivity;
- expected response time;
- geographic or jurisdictional requirements;
- connectivity limitations;
- cost;
- auditability; and
- fallback availability.

### 9.3 In-App Communication

In-app communication may be used when:

- the recipient has an active account;
- the communication does not require a stronger external method;
- display and acknowledgment evidence can be preserved;
- offline or low-connectivity behavior is defined.

### 9.4 Email

Email may be used for:

- operational summaries;
- invoices;
- agreement communications;
- account notices;
- privacy communications;
- formal notices where contract and law permit.

Email addresses used for consequential notices must be verified or otherwise trusted.

### 9.5 SMS

SMS should be used for:

- time-sensitive alerts;
- emergency fallback;
- appointment changes;
- security verification;
- acknowledgment prompts.

Sensitive content should be minimized. SMS must not become the sole repository of critical detail where secure in-app or email content is more appropriate.

### 9.6 Push Notifications

Push notifications are attention mechanisms, not guaranteed evidence of receipt. They should generally direct the recipient to authoritative in-app content.

### 9.7 External Formal Delivery

Where law, contract, or risk requires stronger proof, EquineSync may generate or coordinate:

- certified mail;
- tracked courier;
- process service;
- notarized delivery;
- electronic signature delivery;
- external legal notice service.

EquineSync must not represent ordinary digital delivery as legally sufficient where the required method is uncertain or different.

### 9.8 Channel Fallback

Fallback rules must be defined for material communications.

A typical fallback sequence may include:

1. primary in-app delivery;
2. email;
3. push;
4. SMS;
5. alternate contact;
6. supervisor or guardian;
7. external delivery method;
8. manual intervention.

The exact sequence must be communication-class specific.

---

## 9A. DELIVERY PROOF AND EVIDENCE STANDARDS

### 9A.1 Evidence Tiers

EquineSync should distinguish:

- **attempt evidence:** the system tried to send;
- **provider acceptance evidence:** a provider accepted the request;
- **destination delivery evidence:** the provider reports delivery to a destination or device;
- **display evidence:** the application rendered the content;
- **access evidence:** the recipient opened the authoritative content;
- **acknowledgment evidence:** the recipient affirmatively confirmed awareness;
- **assent evidence:** the recipient affirmatively accepted or authorized;
- **action evidence:** the requested act was completed;
- **external proof evidence:** a third party supplies tracked, certified, witnessed, or service evidence.

### 9A.2 Evidence Reliability

Each evidence type must identify its source, reliability limits, timestamp, and relationship to the recipient. Email pixels and similar telemetry must be treated cautiously because privacy tools, proxies, and automated clients may create false or ambiguous signals.

### 9A.3 Evidence Export

Consequential communication evidence must be exportable in a human-readable package and, where appropriate, a machine-verifiable manifest containing hashes and event chronology.

### 9A.4 Manual Evidence

Authorized users may record phone, in-person, hand-delivery, postal, courier, veterinary, or emergency-service communications. Manual entries must identify the recorder, basis of knowledge, time, participants, summary, supporting documents, and whether the entry is contemporaneous or retrospective.

---

## 10. TIMING AND DEADLINES

### 10.1 Issue Time

Every communication must record the authoritative issue timestamp in Coordinated Universal Time and display recipient-local time where useful.

### 10.2 Effective Time

A communication may have an effective time distinct from the issue time.

### 10.3 Response Window

Where a response is required, the communication must identify:

- the deadline;
- applicable timezone;
- action required;
- consequence of no action;
- extension path, if any;
- contact or dispute path.

### 10.4 Quiet Hours

Preference-eligible communications should respect quiet hours. Mandatory safety, security, welfare, and emergency communications may override quiet hours.

### 10.5 Scheduling

Scheduled communications must preserve:

- creator;
- creation time;
- scheduled send time;
- timezone;
- template version;
- recipient resolution rule;
- cancellation status;
- final recipient list;
- execution evidence.

### 10.6 Recipient Resolution Timing

For role-based or relationship-based communications, the system must define whether recipients are resolved:

- when the communication is created;
- when it is approved;
- when it is sent; or
- dynamically during escalation.

Consequential notices should generally preserve the actual recipient set used at send time.

---

## 11. CONTENT REQUIREMENTS

### 11.1 Minimum Content Elements

Material communications should include, as applicable:

- clear subject or title;
- issuing authority;
- affected horse, facility, agreement, invoice, account, or workflow;
- concise explanation;
- required action;
- deadline;
- consequence;
- contact or response path;
- supporting attachment or link;
- language accessibility;
- version or reference number;
- confidentiality or sensitivity notice;
- dispute or correction path.

### 11.2 Plain-Language Requirement

Communications must be understandable to the intended audience. Legal, medical, financial, and technical content should be stated clearly without sacrificing accuracy.

### 11.3 No Dark Patterns

EquineSync must not:

- hide material consequences;
- preselect consent where prohibited;
- disguise advertising as required notice;
- obscure opt-out rights;
- use misleading urgency;
- make refusal materially harder than acceptance without justification;
- bury critical terms in unrelated text;
- use confusing button labels.

### 11.4 Attachments and Linked Content

Where a communication relies on an attachment or linked document:

- the document must be identifiable;
- the version must be preserved;
- the recipient must have access;
- link expiration must be governed;
- changes after issue must not silently alter the original notice;
- inaccessible attachments must trigger corrective handling.

### 11.5 Templates

Templates must have:

- owner;
- authority level;
- permitted communication classes;
- required fields;
- prohibited modifications;
- review status;
- version;
- effective date;
- expiration or review date;
- localization status;
- accessibility status;
- retention rule.

---

## 12. CONSENT AND COMMUNICATION PREFERENCES

### 12.1 Preference Categories

Recipients may be allowed to configure:

- channel;
- category;
- frequency;
- digest timing;
- quiet hours;
- language;
- accessibility format;
- facility-specific preferences;
- horse-specific preferences;
- marketing participation;
- noncritical reminder frequency.

### 12.2 Mandatory Categories

The following categories may not be fully disabled:

- account security;
- privacy rights;
- legal and regulatory notices;
- contractual notices;
- payment default or account suspension;
- emergency and safety;
- horse welfare;
- critical operational failures;
- consent status;
- service termination.

### 12.3 Consent Evidence

Consent to receive electronic communications must be:

- informed;
- specific enough for the intended purpose;
- recorded;
- attributable;
- revocable where applicable;
- versioned;
- retained.

### 12.4 Withdrawal

Withdrawal must not invalidate communications already lawfully sent or records already required to be retained.

### 12.5 Marketing Separation

Marketing consent must be distinct from consent to receive essential service communications.

---

## 12A. SUPPRESSION, OVERRIDE, AND EXCEPTION GOVERNANCE

### 12A.1 Suppression Sources

A communication may be suppressed only through an authorized source, including:

- recipient preference;
- lawful opt-out;
- duplicate detection;
- completed underlying action;
- withdrawn source event;
- invalid destination;
- relationship termination;
- jurisdictional restriction;
- safety rule preventing disclosure;
- administrator intervention within scope.

### 12A.2 Mandatory Override

An override of preference or quiet hours must record:

- the mandatory communication class;
- the rule authorizing override;
- the actor or system process invoking it;
- the channels used;
- the time;
- the result.

### 12A.3 No Hidden Suppression

The platform must make unresolved suppressed consequential communications visible to authorized operators. Suppression must not function as silent deletion.

### 12A.4 Contact Revocation

Revocation of a destination does not automatically revoke all communications. The system must determine whether another lawful destination or external notice method is required.

---

## 13. ACKNOWLEDGMENT, ACCEPTANCE, AND RESPONSE

### 13.1 Acknowledgment Requirements

Acknowledgment may be required where awareness is materially important, including:

- emergency instructions;
- medication changes;
- horse welfare alerts;
- facility closures;
- safety directives;
- policy acknowledgments;
- legal hold instructions;
- material operational assignments.

### 13.2 Acceptance Requirements

Acceptance is required where the recipient is agreeing to:

- contractual terms;
- payment arrangements;
- authorizations;
- waivers;
- consent;
- delegation;
- service changes;
- policy obligations where affirmative assent is required.

### 13.3 Response Authenticity

The system must preserve:

- responding actor;
- authentication state;
- timestamp;
- content version;
- device or session metadata where lawful and appropriate;
- authority basis;
- response value;
- revocation or supersession state.

### 13.4 No Automatic Conversion

Opening a message must not automatically become acknowledgment. Acknowledgment must not automatically become acceptance. Acceptance must not automatically authorize acts beyond the communicated scope.

---

## 14. ESCALATION MODEL

### 14.1 Escalation Triggers

Escalation may be triggered by:

- delivery failure;
- no acknowledgment;
- missed deadline;
- unresolved safety condition;
- overdue task;
- repeated failure;
- account inactivity;
- recipient unavailability;
- relationship termination;
- emergency severity;
- suspicious activity;
- legal deadline;
- system failure.

### 14.2 Escalation Dimensions

Escalation may increase:

- urgency;
- channel count;
- recipient count;
- supervisory visibility;
- organizational authority;
- human intervention;
- message prominence;
- audit detail;
- response requirement.

### 14.3 Escalation Boundaries

Escalation must not disclose information to a broader audience than necessary. Emergency expansion must remain proportionate.

### 14.4 Human Intervention

The platform must define when automated escalation stops and human intervention becomes mandatory.

### 14.5 Emergency Contact Chains

Emergency chains should support:

1. primary responsible actor;
2. alternate responsible actor;
3. guardian or owner;
4. facility manager;
5. veterinarian or emergency service;
6. platform or organizational administrator where appropriate.

The actual chain must be configurable and relationship-aware.

---

## 15. FAILURE, RETRY, AND DEAD-LETTER HANDLING

### 15.1 Failure Types

The system must distinguish:

- invalid destination;
- blocked destination;
- provider rejection;
- temporary failure;
- permanent failure;
- expired credential;
- user opt-out;
- account closure;
- delivery timeout;
- attachment failure;
- unsupported format;
- channel outage;
- policy suppression;
- jurisdictional restriction.

### 15.2 Retry Rules

Retries must be:

- bounded;
- class-specific;
- channel-aware;
- resistant to duplication;
- recorded;
- stopped when harmful or unlawful.

### 15.3 Dead-Letter State

A communication that cannot be delivered must enter a governed unresolved state rather than disappearing.

### 15.4 Manual Resolution

Consequential failed notices must create a visible task, exception, or escalation for human resolution.

---

## 16. DUPLICATION, BATCHING, AND DIGESTS

### 16.1 Deduplication

The system should prevent unintended duplicates while preserving intentionally repeated reminders or escalations.

### 16.2 Digest Eligibility

Only communications suitable for delayed awareness may be included in digests.

Emergency, safety, security, legal deadline, welfare, and other high-severity communications must not be delayed by digest batching.

### 16.3 Threading

Related communications should be threaded or linked where doing so improves comprehension without obscuring new duties or deadlines.

### 16.4 Supersession

A communication that supersedes another must identify the prior communication and preserve the historical record.

---

## 17. ORGANIZATIONAL AND MULTI-FACILITY COMMUNICATIONS

### 17.1 Organizational Authority

Organizations must control which roles may:

- issue announcements;
- send client messages;
- send billing notices;
- create emergency alerts;
- communicate with minors;
- send legal notices;
- create templates;
- view delivery evidence;
- export communication logs.

### 17.2 Multi-Facility Separation

A user associated with multiple facilities must not receive cross-facility information unless the relationship and permission model authorizes it.

### 17.3 Shared Horses and Shared Services

When multiple organizations or professionals are involved with one horse, communication must respect:

- ownership;
- custody;
- care responsibility;
- treatment authority;
- contractual limits;
- privacy;
- emergency authorization;
- service-provider scope.

### 17.4 Transfer Continuity

Upon horse transfer, facility change, account migration, or organizational succession, communication continuity must follow the Horse Transfer and Passport Continuity Model and applicable record governance.

---

## 18. SERVICE PROVIDERS AND EXTERNAL PARTICIPANTS

Veterinarians, farriers, bodyworkers, chiropractors, haulers, braiders, trainers, photographers, feed suppliers, and other service providers may receive communications only within authorized scope.

The platform must support:

- appointment requests;
- service confirmations;
- access instructions;
- care notes;
- invoices;
- follow-up instructions;
- documentation requests;
- emergency contact;
- limited temporary access;
- revocation after service completion.

External participants must not receive broad facility or horse records merely because they receive a communication.

---

## 19. BILLING AND PAYMENT NOTICES

Financial notices must distinguish among:

- invoice issued;
- upcoming due date;
- payment received;
- payment failed;
- payment reversed;
- payment disputed;
- account overdue;
- cure notice;
- payment plan;
- service suspension warning;
- suspension;
- reinstatement;
- credit issued;
- refund issued.

Material financial notices must include:

- amount;
- currency;
- obligation source;
- due date;
- payment status;
- consequence;
- available payment methods;
- dispute path;
- contact path;
- applicable agreement reference.

EquineSync must not label a default, cure, suspension, or termination notice as a mere reminder.

---

## 20. SAFETY, WELFARE, AND EMERGENCY COMMUNICATIONS

### 20.1 Priority

Safety, welfare, and emergency communications receive the highest routing priority.

### 20.2 Required Elements

Emergency communications should identify:

- affected horse or person;
- nature of emergency;
- location;
- observed time;
- immediate actions taken;
- requested response;
- responsible contact;
- veterinary or emergency service status;
- authorization status;
- update cadence.

### 20.3 Progressive Updates

Emergency workflows should support:

- initial alert;
- acknowledgment;
- status update;
- escalation;
- resolution;
- after-action summary.

### 20.4 No Silent Failure

Any delivery failure in an active emergency workflow must be surfaced immediately to a responsible human.

---

## 20A. INCIDENT, CRISIS, AND PUBLIC COMMUNICATIONS

### 20A.1 Incident Communication Plan

Material safety, welfare, privacy, security, service, or operational incidents must have a controlled communication plan identifying:

- incident commander or accountable authority;
- internal recipients;
- affected external recipients;
- regulators or emergency services, where applicable;
- approved facts;
- prohibited speculation;
- update cadence;
- channel sequence;
- spokesperson;
- closure communication;
- evidence retention.

### 20A.2 Single Source of Truth

Incident communications must derive from an authoritative incident record. Conflicting local messages must be corrected or superseded promptly.

### 20A.3 Public Statements

Public statements, social posts, press responses, and broad community announcements concerning a material incident require explicit authority and must not expose confidential, personal, medical, financial, security, or legally restricted information.

### 20A.4 Post-Incident Review

After resolution, the organization should review communication timing, accuracy, recipient coverage, failures, escalation, and lessons learned.

---

## 21. LEGAL AND FORMAL NOTICE GOVERNANCE

### 21.1 Legal Sufficiency

EquineSync must not declare a notice legally effective solely because it was created or sent through the platform.

Legal sufficiency depends on:

- governing law;
- contract;
- jurisdiction;
- recipient;
- content;
- delivery method;
- timing;
- evidence;
- applicable consent to electronic delivery.

### 21.2 Legal Review Triggers

Legal review should be required for templates or workflows involving:

- termination;
- default;
- cure;
- breach;
- indemnity;
- waiver;
- release;
- mandatory policy changes;
- privacy notice;
- data breach;
- litigation hold;
- regulatory reporting;
- formal demand;
- disputed ownership;
- horse seizure or abandonment;
- high-risk safety events.

### 21.3 Exact Preservation

The exact issued content, attachments, recipient list, delivery path, timestamps, and outcome must be preserved.

### 21.4 Jurisdictional Variance

The platform must support jurisdiction-specific rules without treating one jurisdiction’s notice standard as universal.

---

## 21A. COMMUNICATION SAFETY, HARASSMENT, AND MISUSE CONTROLS

EquineSync must protect communication surfaces from:

- harassment;
- threats;
- stalking behavior;
- impersonation;
- coercive messaging;
- spam;
- fraudulent payment requests;
- unauthorized solicitation;
- disclosure of minors’ information;
- malicious attachments or links;
- abuse of emergency channels;
- retaliation through organizational messaging powers.

Controls should include proportionate rate limits, reporting, blocking, moderation, administrator review, evidence preservation, emergency escalation, and appeal mechanisms.

Blocking an individual must not prevent delivery of a separately lawful and mandatory organizational, safety, legal, or account notice. Such notices should use controlled nonconversational channels and prevent unauthorized conversational reentry.

---

## 22. PRIVACY, CONFIDENTIALITY, AND SECURITY

### 22.1 Data Minimization

Communications must contain only necessary personal, financial, medical, horse-care, or legal information.

### 22.2 Sensitive Content

Sensitive content should be placed behind authenticated access when feasible, with the external message containing only a minimal prompt.

### 22.3 Contact Verification

Consequential communications must use verified or trusted contact destinations.

### 22.4 Shared Devices and Shared Inboxes

The platform must account for the risk of shared devices, shared email accounts, and shared organizational inboxes.

### 22.5 Security Logging

Security-sensitive communication events must be logged and protected from unauthorized alteration.

### 22.6 Redaction

Exports, support views, and administrative tools must redact communication content where full visibility is unnecessary.

---

## 23. ACCESSIBILITY, LANGUAGE, AND COMPREHENSION

EquineSync communications must be designed for accessibility.

The system should support:

- screen readers;
- keyboard navigation;
- sufficient contrast;
- plain language;
- scalable text;
- descriptive links;
- accessible attachments;
- captioned media;
- language preferences;
- localization;
- translation workflows;
- alternative formats.

Translated content must preserve legal and operational meaning. Machine translation may assist but must not be treated as authoritative for high-risk legal, safety, or medical communications without appropriate review.

---

## 24. OFFLINE AND LOW-CONNECTIVITY BEHAVIOR

EquineSync must define how communications behave when:

- the user is offline;
- the device is locked;
- the application is suspended;
- the network is intermittent;
- the channel provider is unavailable;
- content has not fully synchronized.

Critical communications should:

- queue safely;
- preserve issue and delivery times;
- synchronize when connectivity returns;
- avoid duplicate acknowledgment;
- indicate stale information;
- support fallback channels;
- preserve the authoritative server state.

---

## 25. THIRD-PARTY SERVICES AND INTEGRATIONS

### 25.1 Approved Providers

External communication providers may be used only after governed review of:

- security;
- privacy;
- data processing;
- retention;
- deliverability;
- reliability;
- jurisdiction;
- subprocessor use;
- audit evidence;
- cost;
- incident response;
- contractual terms.

### 25.2 Provider Independence

EquineSync’s communication record must not depend entirely on a third-party provider dashboard.

### 25.3 Provider Metadata

The system should preserve provider identifiers, status codes, timestamps, failure reasons, and webhook evidence.

### 25.4 Integration Authority

An integration may trigger a communication only within expressly granted scope.

### 25.5 Webhook Authenticity

Inbound delivery and status webhooks must be authenticated, idempotent, and resistant to replay.

---

## 26. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND AUTOMATION

### 26.1 No Independent Authority by Default

Artificial intelligence does not possess independent authority to issue binding, legal, medical, financial, safety-critical, or disciplinary communications unless a future canon expressly grants limited authority.

### 26.2 Permitted Assistance

Future authorized AI may assist with:

- drafting;
- summarization;
- translation;
- recipient suggestions;
- urgency detection;
- template selection;
- duplicate detection;
- digest generation;
- delivery-failure triage.

### 26.3 Human Review

High-risk communications must require human review unless a later canon approves a bounded exception.

### 26.4 Disclosure

Recipients should be informed when material content was generated or materially transformed by AI where relevant to trust, accuracy, or legal compliance.

### 26.5 Auditability

AI-assisted communication must preserve:

- source inputs;
- model or system version where required;
- generated draft;
- human edits;
- approving actor;
- final content;
- delivery state.

## 27A. SYSTEM-OF-RECORD AND CANONICAL DATA RULES

### 27A.1 Authoritative Record

EquineSync must identify which store is authoritative for:

- communication content;
- recipient instances;
- delivery attempts;
- provider events;
- acknowledgment and assent;
- thread presentation;
- attachments;
- evidence manifests;
- retention state.

### 27A.2 Event Ordering

Events arriving out of order must be reconciled without falsifying chronology. Provider time, platform receipt time, and authoritative recorded time should remain distinguishable.

### 27A.3 Clock and Timezone Integrity

The platform must use synchronized server time for authoritative events, preserve UTC, and retain the timezone used for recipient-facing deadlines.

### 27A.4 Content Integrity

Material communication content and attachments should be hashable so later evidence can demonstrate whether the retained package matches the issued package.

## 28. RETENTION, LEGAL HOLD, AND DISPOSAL

Communication records are governed by the Master Record Stewardship and Retention Model.

- communication class;
- underlying relationship;
- contract;
- payment record;
- safety incident;
- horse medical history;
- legal matter;
- privacy request;
- security event;
- regulatory obligation;
- dispute;
- applicable limitation period.

Messages must not be automatically deleted merely because a user removes them from a personal view if the platform or organization is required to retain them.

### 29.1 Correction

- identify that a correction has been issued;

- preserve the original;

### 29.2 Withdrawal

- withdrawing authority;
- reason;
- time;
- affected recipients;

### 29.3 Revocation

Revocation of consent, authorization, or delegation must trigger communication and downstream state changes where required.

### 29.4 Erroneous Recipients

If a communication is sent to an unauthorized recipient, the event must be treated as a privacy and security incident according to applicable policy.

- applicable ATLAS governance;
- applicable RF planning and implementation gates.

Where communication involves a conflict among permissions, relationships, consent, ownership, stewardship, and notice obligations, the issue must be resolved through the applicable constitutional documents rather than by local feature logic.

## 36. PROHIBITED PRACTICES

EquineSync must not:

- treat all messages as legally equivalent;
- infer receipt from transmission alone;
- permit unauthorized mass messaging;
- allow users to disable mandatory notices;
- use push notifications as the sole proof of consequential notice;
- silently alter previously issued content;
- send sensitive content to unverified destinations without justification;
- expose recipient lists unnecessarily;
- use marketing channels for emergency communications;
- disguise marketing as operational notice;
- treat a read event as acceptance;
- permit integrations to send beyond granted scope;
- permit AI to independently issue high-risk communications without authority;
- delete evidence subject to retention or legal hold;
- allow local modules to invent conflicting notice standards;
- create communication obligations without identifying the responsible actor;
- rely solely on third-party provider records;
- suppress delivery failures;
- allow unresolved critical alerts to disappear;
- use vague deadlines without timezone clarity;
- issue formal notices from ambiguous or misleading sender identities.

## 40. CANONICAL RULE

EquineSync shall treat communication, notification, and notice as distinct but connected governed actions.

No communication may be considered complete merely because text was generated.

A consequential communication is complete only when the platform has:

1. identified a valid authority;
2. identified the proper recipient;
3. classified the communication correctly;
4. selected an appropriate channel;
5. preserved the issued content;
6. recorded transmission and delivery evidence;
7. obtained acknowledgment, acceptance, or response where required;
8. escalated failure where required; and
9. retained the resulting record according to canon.
