# 1. Constitutional Purpose

This Model establishes the constitutional framework for the creation, receipt, capture, import, storage, classification, linking, access, transformation, transmission, sharing, indexing, search, versioning, retention, preservation, export, recovery, restriction, and disposition of media, files, documents, recordings, images, video, audio, diagnostic imaging, scans, digital evidence, system-generated objects, AI-generated content, AI-modified content, previews, thumbnails, transcripts, extracted text, metadata, indexes, annotations, signatures, and related digital assets within or through EquineSync.

The Model converts founder-approved decisions into durable platform requirements. It governs product design, engineering, mobile and offline behavior, administrative tools, customer support, external integrations, vendor processing, security operations, evidence handling, migration, business continuity, and future digital-asset capabilities.

This Model is intended to make EquineSync capable of answering, for any material asset:

1. What is it?
2. Where did it come from?
3. Who introduced it?
4. Who owns or controls relevant rights?
5. Who is its steward or custodian?
6. Which horse, person, facility, organization, event, claim, or record is it related to?
7. What classification and sensitivity apply?
8. Is it authoritative, evidentiary, historic, temporary, disputed, or merely informational?
9. Has it changed?
10. What derived objects exist?
11. Who may see, download, transform, share, or export it?
12. Which retention, hold, continuity, or deletion rules apply?
13. What must happen when access, ownership, custody, consent, or account status changes?
14. Can its integrity and lineage be proven?
15. What authority, if any, does it create?

If EquineSync cannot answer these questions for a material asset, the asset is not adequately governed.

## 1.1 Objectives

This Model shall:

- establish Digital Asset as a first-class constitutional domain;
- protect horse, person, organization, and facility information from unauthorized exposure;
- preserve originals and material provenance;
- prevent silent overwriting, silent relabeling, and silent authority expansion;
- distinguish ownership, stewardship, custody, possession, access, and reuse rights;
- protect minors, guardians, workers, providers, and non-account holders;
- support horse-history continuity without transferring unrelated private context;
- establish reliable evidentiary and chain-of-custody controls;
- govern AI, OCR, transcription, enhancement, and synthetic media;
- support offline and low-connectivity workflows safely;
- preserve portability and provider neutrality;
- establish secure sharing, export, migration, and vendor-exit requirements;
- support accessibility without weakening truth, privacy, or evidence;
- ensure derived assets inherit appropriate controls;
- and create implementation gates capable of proving constitutional compliance before production activation.

## 1.2 Constitutional Function

This Model governs the constitutional meaning and treatment of digital assets. It does not prescribe every technical file format, storage provider, compression setting, upload limit, codec, scanning engine, image dimension, or implementation library.

Those matters belong in subordinate technical standards, architecture decisions, implementation specifications, and controlled matrices, provided they remain consistent with this Model.

Technical convenience may not redefine constitutional ownership, authority, evidence, privacy, retention, or access.

# 3. Foundational Constitutional Principles

## 3.1 Digital Asset as a First-Class Domain

The constitutional object is the Digital Asset, not merely the stored binary.

A Digital Asset shall be capable of possessing:

- a durable identity;
- one or more binary or logical representations;
- provenance;
- classification;
- sensitivity;
- rights information;
- stewardship and custody;
- version lineage;
- relationships;
- permissions;
- evidence state;
- retention obligations;
- audit history;
- integrity evidence;
- AI and transformation lineage;
- and lifecycle state.

## 3.2 Private by Default

Assets are private unless a lawful, authorized, purpose-specific rule permits broader access.

Upload is not publication. Association with a public profile is not publication. Inclusion in a shared barn is not publication. Visibility to one role is not visibility to all roles.

## 3.3 Minimum Necessary Access

Actors may access only the minimum content, metadata, version, relationship, and functionality necessary for an authorized purpose.

The ability to view an asset does not automatically include the ability to download, edit metadata, view all versions, see private annotations, inspect hidden metadata, export, share, or grant access to others.

## 3.4 Original Preservation

Originals shall be preserved when required by classification, evidence, medical significance, legal significance, horse-history continuity, or retention governance.

Derived copies shall not silently replace originals.

## 3.5 No Silent Mutation

No material asset, metadata element, classification, authority status, evidence status, version relationship, access rule, or retention state may be silently changed.

Material changes must be attributable, authorized, and auditable.

## 3.6 Possession Does Not Create Authority

Possessing, uploading, downloading, storing, linking, forwarding, or controlling the technical location of an asset does not by itself establish:

- ownership;
- authorship;
- authenticity;
- authority;
- custody rights;
- consent;
- public-use rights;
- marketing rights;
- or truth.

## 3.7 Technical Integrity Is Not Factual Truth

A valid hash proves consistency with a known byte sequence. It does not prove the content is accurate, authentic, lawfully created, or correctly attributed.

File integrity, source attribution, identity verification, issuer verification, signature validation, content authenticity, and factual accuracy are separate claims.

## 3.8 Continuity Without Overexposure

Horse continuity shall preserve necessary horse history without transferring unrelated private, privileged, personnel, safeguarding, commercial, or operational context.

## 3.9 Derived Controls Follow the Source

Thumbnails, previews, transcripts, OCR, embeddings, redactions, translations, summaries, and extracted data must inherit the source asset's applicable privacy, security, retention, evidence, and access restrictions unless a stricter rule applies.

## 3.10 Safe Failure

When authority, integrity, classification, source, malware status, synchronization state, or retention obligation is uncertain, the platform shall fail safely through restriction, quarantine, deferral, or human review rather than silent availability or destruction.

## 3.11 Provider Neutrality

No storage, scanning, streaming, AI, OCR, signature, or media-processing provider may become constitutionally authoritative merely because it is technically central.

## 3.12 Human Accountability

AI and automation may assist with media workflows, but accountable human and organizational authority remains required for consequential designation, disclosure, deletion, legal hold, evidence treatment, medical interpretation, ownership, safeguarding, and dispute resolution.

# 4. Definitions

For purposes of this Model:

## 4.1 Digital Asset

A governed digital object or logical collection of objects having identity, provenance, relationships, rights, permissions, lifecycle, and retention treatment.

## 4.2 Original Asset

The initially captured, received, issued, imported, or uploaded object preserved as the primary source representation.

## 4.3 Derived Asset

An output created from another asset through transformation, including compression, cropping, transcoding, annotation, OCR, transcription, translation, redaction, enhancement, summarization, extraction, watermarking, or AI processing.

## 4.4 Binary Object

The stored sequence of bytes or comparable technical object representing file content.

## 4.5 Logical Asset

A governed asset whose constitutional identity may include one or more binaries, pages, media segments, attachments, manifests, signature envelopes, or representations.

## 4.6 Asset Identifier

A durable, non-semantic identifier uniquely referencing the governed asset.

## 4.7 Content Hash

A cryptographic digest used to verify that content matches a known byte state.

## 4.8 Provenance

The record of origin, receipt, creation, issuer, uploader, source system, transformations, versions, custody, and material handling history.

## 4.9 Rights Holder

A person or organization holding copyright, contractual, privacy, publicity, confidentiality, ownership, or related rights.

## 4.10 Steward

A person or organization responsible for classification, record association, preservation, access administration, or lifecycle governance without necessarily owning the asset.

## 4.11 Custodian

A person or organization possessing or controlling an asset for an authorized purpose.

## 4.12 Uploader

The actor or system that introduces an asset into EquineSync.

## 4.13 Issuer

The person or organization responsible for formally issuing a document, certificate, report, image set, or record.

## 4.14 Subject

A person, horse, facility, organization, or other entity depicted, described, identified, or materially affected by an asset.

## 4.15 Authoritative Record

A record accepted as controlling or officially reliable for a defined scope under an approved process.

## 4.16 Evidence-Designated Asset

An asset assigned enhanced evidentiary treatment through a governed process.

## 4.17 Potential Evidence

An asset preserved because it may become relevant to a claim, incident, investigation, or proceeding but has not been verified or formally accepted.

## 4.18 Historic Asset

An asset designated for long-term preservation because of enduring horse, organizational, legal, cultural, or operational value.

## 4.19 Transitory Asset

A temporary object not intended to become a permanent record, subject to controlled expiration.

## 4.20 Redaction

A governed concealment or removal of content from a derived copy while preserving the original where required and permitted.

## 4.21 De-Identification

A governed process reducing direct or indirect linkability to an identifiable person, horse, organization, facility, or location.

## 4.22 Quarantine

A restricted state preventing ordinary use while safety, integrity, rights, or classification issues are resolved.

## 4.23 Legal or Preservation Hold

A control suspending disposition or incompatible transformation because of legal, regulatory, claim, safeguarding, investigation, insurance, or other preservation duties.

## 4.24 Asset Relationship

A governed association between an asset and one or more horses, people, facilities, organizations, events, claims, invoices, treatments, tasks, incidents, agreements, messages, or records.

## 4.25 Asset State

The current governed lifecycle condition of an asset.

## 4.26 Technical Transformation

A processing step intended to alter representation without changing constitutional meaning, such as preview generation or transcoding.

## 4.27 Material Transformation

A processing step capable of changing interpretation, evidentiary weight, apparent content, accessibility, or meaning.

## 4.28 Restricted Metadata

Metadata not available through ordinary access because it contains sensitive, privileged, location, device, evidence, or administrative information.

## 4.29 Share Link

A revocable access mechanism permitting an authorized recipient to access defined content outside ordinary in-platform relationships.

## 4.30 Evidence Export

A controlled export preserving selected assets, metadata, hashes, relationships, and manifests for verification or external proceedings.

## 4.31 Orphaned Asset

An asset lacking a valid owner, steward, relationship, tenant, account, or lifecycle path and requiring reconciliation.

## 4.32 Synthetic Media

Media generated or materially altered by computational systems so that it may depict or communicate content not captured in the represented event.

## 4.33 Deepfake

Synthetic or manipulated media designed or capable of misleading viewers about identity, speech, conduct, event occurrence, condition, authority, or source.

# 5. Asset Identity and Object Model

## 5.1 Durable Identity

Every permanent or governed asset shall receive a durable asset identifier independent of:

- filename;
- storage path;
- provider key;
- user-facing title;
- horse name;
- account email;
- or external system identifier.

Identifiers shall not be reassigned.

## 5.2 Single Asset, Multiple Relationships

A single asset may be related to multiple entities without duplicating the underlying binary object.

A radiograph may relate to a horse, veterinarian, medical visit, injury, claim, owner, and insurance submission. These relationships do not need separate binary copies unless required for isolation, export, redaction, regional control, or retention.

## 5.3 Relationship Does Not Equal Access

The existence of a relationship does not create access by itself.

Access must be evaluated using the actor's identity, role, relationship, purpose, consent, asset classification, metadata restrictions, and current authorization.

## 5.4 Asset-Level and Relationship-Level Context

The platform shall distinguish:

- the underlying asset;
- relationship-specific captions or annotations;
- private notes;
- version visibility;
- classification context;
- and relationship-specific access.

An actor authorized through one relationship may not automatically see another relationship's private context.

## 5.5 No Filename Authority

Filenames are descriptive convenience, not proof of identity, issuer, date, classification, or authenticity.

## 5.6 Asset Bundles

Logical bundles, including veterinary study packages, agreement packages, evidence collections, and multi-page records, shall preserve:

- bundle identity;
- component identity;
- component order;
- completeness status;
- missing-component status;
- version relationship;
- and manifest integrity.

# 6. Ownership, Rights, Stewardship, and Custody

## 6.1 Intellectual Property Ownership

The creator, photographer, author, issuer, employer, commissioning party, assignee, or other lawful rights holder retains intellectual-property ownership according to law and contract.

Upload, import, synchronization, or storage does not transfer ownership to EquineSync.

## 6.2 Limited Platform License

EquineSync receives only the limited, non-exclusive rights reasonably necessary to:

- receive;
- validate;
- scan;
- store;
- encrypt;
- replicate;
- back up;
- recover;
- transform for technical delivery;
- display to authorized actors;
- synchronize;
- index;
- search;
- export;
- preserve;
- audit;
- and administer the asset under controlling governance.

No broader commercialization or sublicensing right arises merely from storage.

## 6.3 Distinct Rights and Roles

The system shall distinguish, where applicable:

- copyright owner;
- contractual owner;
- data controller or business decision-maker;
- data processor or service provider;
- subject;
- uploader;
- issuer;
- steward;
- custodian;
- authorized viewer;
- authorized editor;
- preservation authority;
- and disposition authority.

## 6.4 Multiple Rights Holders

An asset may have multiple rights holders or subjects. Equal association does not imply equal authority.

Disputed rights shall be preserved and escalated rather than resolved automatically.

## 6.5 Stewardship

Stewardship may include responsibility for:

- classification;
- relationship management;
- access review;
- issuer verification;
- retention assignment;
- correction workflow;
- preservation;
- and dispute routing.

Stewardship shall not be treated as ownership.

## 6.6 Custody

Custody records who possesses or controls the asset at a given time or within a given workflow.

Custody shall be material for evidence, claims, provider records, transfer, exports, and external processing.

## 6.7 Rights Representations

Uploaders and importers shall represent, to the extent reasonably applicable, that introduction of the asset is authorized.

EquineSync shall not rely solely on this representation when heightened verification is warranted.

## 6.8 Rights Complaints

EquineSync shall maintain a controlled process for copyright, privacy, publicity, confidentiality, privilege, mistaken identity, unauthorized publication, forgery, and related complaints.

Available responses may include:

- restriction;
- quarantine;
- preservation;
- notice;
- redaction;
- access suspension;
- controlled takedown;
- and dispute annotation.

## 6.9 No Automatic Destruction

A rights complaint shall not trigger automatic destruction where legal hold, evidence, safeguarding, fraud, horse history, or other preservation duties apply.

## 8.2 State Transition Authority

Each transition shall define:

- permitted actors;
- required authority;
- prerequisites;
- automated or human review;
- audit evidence;
- downstream effects;
- and reversal or appeal treatment.

## 8.3 No Silent State Change

No material state transition may occur silently.

## 8.4 Pending and Incomplete Assets

Incomplete uploads shall not be presented as complete records.

## 8.5 Rejected Assets

Rejected assets shall retain enough evidence to explain the rejection without unnecessarily preserving prohibited or dangerous content.

## 8.6 Quarantined Assets

Quarantined assets shall be unavailable to ordinary users and processed only by authorized systems or personnel.

## 8.7 Restored Assets

Recovery-restored assets shall be validated for identity, integrity, version, classification, permissions, relationships, and audit continuity before ordinary activation.

## 8.8 Orphaned Assets

Ownerless, relationship-less, tenant-less, or account-less assets shall enter reconciliation. They shall not remain indefinitely accessible or ungoverned.

# 9. Ingestion, Upload, Capture, and Import

## 9.1 Governed Ingestion

Every ingestion pathway shall preserve:

- initiating actor or system;
- source;
- received time;
- claimed creation time where available;
- filename;
- format;
- size;
- hash;
- initial classification;
- intended relationships;
- and scanning state.

## 9.2 Upload Authorization

An actor may upload only within an authorized workflow and purpose.

Upload authority does not automatically include authority to classify as authoritative, evidence, public, transferable, or historic.

## 9.3 Mobile Capture

Mobile capture shall preserve original media, local creation context, user identity, device trust state, capture time where reliable, location treatment, offline status, and synchronization outcome.

## 9.4 Scanner and Camera Capture

Scanner and camera workflows shall distinguish:

- original physical document;
- captured digital representation;
- capture quality;
- missing pages;
- skew or truncation;
- and whether the capture has been verified.

## 9.5 Email and Message Imports

Attachments imported from email or messaging shall preserve available sender, message, subject, received time, thread, and attachment context without treating those values as conclusive authenticity evidence.

## 9.6 Cloud and Third-Party Imports

Imports from drives, portals, registries, or external systems shall preserve available provenance.

External sharing permissions shall not automatically map to EquineSync access.

## 9.7 External Provider Uploads

Authorized providers may upload through governed workflows. Provider upload authority shall be limited by purpose, relationship, data category, and time.

## 9.8 Batch Imports

Batch imports shall support:

- dry-run validation;
- manifest review;
- duplicate detection;
- classification mapping;
- failure isolation;
- reconciliation;
- and rollback or correction without silent loss.

## 9.9 Failed and Abandoned Uploads

The platform shall govern partial uploads, retries, offline duplication, abandoned sessions, orphaned chunks, and temporary data expiration.

## 9.10 Unsupported and Encrypted Files

Unsupported, password-protected, or encrypted files shall not bypass scanning or classification. They may be rejected, quarantined, or admitted only through a controlled exception.

# 10. Original Preservation and Versioning

## 10.1 Immutable Original Principle

Where preservation applies, the original received or captured asset shall remain unchanged.

## 10.2 New Version Instead of Overwrite

A revised file shall create a new version rather than replace the prior content.

## 10.3 Version Lineage

Version lineage shall preserve:

- parent or prior version;
- actor;
- reason;
- creation time;
- effective time;
- supersession status;
- and material differences where available.

## 10.4 Metadata Revision

Metadata edits shall be distinguished from content versions.

## 10.5 Annotation Revision

Annotations shall have their own authorship, visibility, lifecycle, and revision history.

## 10.6 Redaction Versions

Redacted copies are derived assets, not replacements for the source.

## 10.7 Technical Transformation

Technical transformations such as thumbnail generation, compression, or transcoding shall preserve source linkage and not imply substantive revision.

## 10.8 Correction

Correction shall preserve prior content and explain what changed, why, by whom, and under what authority.

## 10.9 Supersession

Superseded records shall remain discoverable to authorized actors where necessary to understand history, but ordinary views may prioritize the current effective version.

## 11.4 Restriction Propagation

## 11.5 OCR and Transcript Limitations

OCR and transcripts shall be labeled as extracted or transcribed content. They shall not silently become authoritative over the original.

## 11.6 Translation Limitations

Translations shall preserve the source language and disclose whether the translation was human, machine-assisted, or machine-generated.

## 11.7 Extracted Structured Data

Extracted fields shall preserve source linkage and confidence or validation status. Extraction does not automatically create authoritative structured truth.

## 11.8 Embeddings

Embeddings and similar machine representations shall be governed as derived assets. They shall not bypass deletion, access, tenant isolation, or training restrictions.

## 12.1 Metadata Classes

Metadata shall be classified as:

- immutable;
- protected;
- editable;
- calculated;
- inferred;
- externally sourced;
- restricted;
- or transitory.

## 12.2 Immutable or Protected Metadata

Where available, protected metadata shall include:

- asset identifier;
- original upload, issue, import, or capture timestamp;
- originating actor;
- original filename;
- content hash;
- source system;
- original format;
- initial version lineage;
- issuer;

## 12.3 Editable Metadata

Captions, display names, tags, categories, and non-authoritative annotations may be edited through governed processes.

## 12.4 Metadata History

Material metadata revisions must preserve prior value, new value, actor, authority, reason, and time.

## 12.5 Hidden and Sensitive Metadata

GPS, device identifiers, usernames, network information, document properties, revision history, embedded comments, and hidden layers shall be restricted or removed from ordinary sharing by default.

## 12.6 Metadata Reliability

The platform shall distinguish claimed, observed, calculated, verified, and inferred metadata.

## 12.7 Timestamp Semantics

Upload time, capture time, issue time, source-system time, synchronization time, and modification time shall remain distinct.

Synchronization time shall not replace original action or capture time.

# 13. Integrity, Authenticity, and Provenance

## 13.1 Cryptographic Integrity

Permanent assets shall receive a cryptographic hash using SHA-256 or an approved successor.

## 13.2 Repeatable Verification

Integrity verification shall be repeatable during storage, backup, migration, restore, and export.

## 13.3 Provenance Record

Material provenance shall preserve:

- origin;
- uploader;
- issuer;
- source system;
- receipt;
- transformations;
- versions;
- custody;
- access where required;
- exports;
- and disposition.

## 13.4 Authenticity Is Multi-Part

EquineSync shall not collapse file integrity, source attribution, identity, issuer authority, signature validity, content authenticity, and factual accuracy into a single label.

## 13.5 Forgery and Manipulation

Suspected forgery, tampering, metadata falsification, or synthetic manipulation shall trigger restriction, preservation, and review appropriate to risk.

## 13.6 Watermarks

Watermarks may indicate source, recipient, classification, or sharing status, but shall not be represented as proof of authenticity by themselves.

## 13.7 Content Credentials and Future Standards

EquineSync may support content credentials or comparable provenance standards where they improve traceability. Their presence shall not override independent verification.

# 14. Authoritative Record Governance

## 14.1 No Automatic Authority

Upload, storage, filename, format, user label, or provider logo does not automatically create authoritative status.

## 14.2 Required Basis

Authoritative designation shall require defined:

- record scope;
- issuer category;
- validation;
- workflow;
- completeness;
- effective date;
- responsible authority;
- and supersession rules.

## 14.3 Scope-Specific Authority

An asset may be authoritative for one fact but not another.

A veterinary certificate may be authoritative as to the issuing veterinarian's statement, but not necessarily as to every underlying factual assumption.

## 14.4 Scans and Copies

A scan may be an authoritative representation only where the governing workflow recognizes the copy and records its status.

## 14.5 OCR Is Not Authority

OCR text is not authoritative over the source image unless verified and separately accepted.

## 14.6 Revocation and Withdrawal

Authority may be revoked, superseded, corrected, or disputed through a governed process without erasing historical status.

## 14.7 Conflicting Records

Conflicting authoritative or potentially authoritative assets shall be preserved, annotated, and reconciled. The platform shall not silently choose the newest or most convenient asset unless controlling rules authorize that resolution.

# 54. Cross-Canon Reconciliation and Domain Ownership Rules

## 54.1 Domain Ownership

Each constitutional domain retains primary authority over the subject matter assigned to it by the Constitutional Domain Ownership and Boundary Register. This Model owns Digital Asset identity, asset versions, originals and derivatives, provenance and lineage, asset-specific lifecycle, asset relationships, media-specific classification, transformation, publication, and asset-domain handling rules.

This Model does not independently own final access authorization, privacy-law processing authority, record retention, legal holds, cryptographic key lifecycle, security-incident disclosure, agreement validity, claims adjudication, or platform recovery doctrine. Those subjects remain controlled by their specialized constitutional owners.

## 54.2 Specificity Within the Owning Domain

Within an assigned domain, the more specific controlling rule governs over a general rule. A specialized rule may narrow a general allowance but may not silently expand authority beyond the owning canon.

## 54.3 Compatible Protective Rules

Where two valid rules can coexist, both apply. A more protective rule may supplement another rule when it does not usurp the assigned authority of another constitutional owner.

## 54.4 Safeguarding, Law, Court Orders, and Holds

Applicable law, valid court orders, safeguarding duties, emergency protective controls, and valid legal or preservation holds may require immediate restriction, preservation, notice, or escalation. Their protective effect does not convert one domain into universal authority over unrelated constitutional subjects.

## 54.5 Irreconcilable Conflict

Where two controlling rules cannot reasonably coexist, implementation, migration, activation, or destructive action must pause to the extent necessary. The conflict must be recorded and resolved through the Constitutional Domain Ownership and Boundary Register, Cross-Canon Reference Normalization Register, Constitutional Authority Matrix, and controlled governance review.

No implementer, administrator, provider, integration, or automated system may choose a preferred rule based on convenience, cost, provider behavior, or technical architecture.

## 54.6 No Blanket Exception

No rule, exception, role, consent, provider capability, or customer request creates a blanket exception to specialized constitutional protections.

## 54.7 Constitutional Family References

Where titles or versions change, the active controlling artifact identified by the Canon Index and newest-version acceptance rule governs after controlled reconciliation. Legacy titles are aliases only and must not create competing authority.

Retirement of a media service, file type, provider, feature, or the EquineSync service itself shall be governed through a documented plan.

## 67.2 Required Elements

The plan shall address:

- notice;
- export;
- portability;
- horse continuity;
- legal holds;
- active claims;
- public links;
- offline copies;
- processor deletion;
- residual backups;
- cryptographic keys;
- audit preservation;
- and customer support.

## 67.3 No Abandonment

Protected or historic assets shall not become inaccessible merely because a feature or provider is discontinued.

## 67.4 Format Obsolescence

Long-term assets shall be monitored for format and codec obsolescence. Preservation migration shall retain originals and document transformation.

## 67.5 Final Deletion Evidence

Where destruction is authorized at end of service, EquineSync shall preserve evidence sufficient to support the deletion claim without retaining the deleted content itself.

# 68. Final Constitutional Statement

EquineSync may store files, but it must govern assets.

A digital object may carry the history of a horse, the privacy of a child, the judgment of a veterinarian, the rights of a photographer, the evidence of an incident, the terms of an agreement, or the memory of a lifetime.

Those meanings cannot be flattened into a filename and a download button.

Every material asset must remain attributable, bounded, reviewable, portable, and protected. Its original must not disappear beneath convenience. Its history must not be rewritten by transformation. Its relationships must not become a tunnel through authorization. Its appearance must not be mistaken for truth. Its usefulness must not become permission for secondary exploitation.

EquineSync may make digital assets easier to use. It may not make them easier to misuse.
