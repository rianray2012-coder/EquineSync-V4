# Purpose and Constitutional Scope

This Model governs an equestrian business as a durable organizational and commercial entity from formation through operation, growth, restructuring, succession, insolvency, closure, and archive.

A business is not merely a billing profile or decorative account wrapper. It may own contracts, employ people, provide services, operate across facilities, maintain reputation, receive funds, incur obligations, and outlive individual users.

# Business Identity Rule

- Business identity must remain distinct from owner identity, user accounts, legal entities, brands, facilities, merchant accounts, marketplace profiles, and provider credentials.
- One legal entity may operate multiple brands or business units; one brand may be used by more than one legal arrangement only when explicitly governed.
- Business continuity must not depend on a single founder, employee, email address, device, or payment-provider account.
- Changes in beneficial ownership, management, legal form, tax status, or operating name must preserve historical truth and require appropriate verification.

# Business Lifecycle Stages

1. Concept and pre-formation planning
2. Legal formation, tax, banking, insurance, licensing, and governance setup
3. Platform onboarding, authority verification, service configuration, and initial customer relationships
4. Active operations, service delivery, workforce, billing, reporting, and compliance
5. Growth, multi-location operation, marketplace participation, financing, or restructuring
6. Ownership change, merger, acquisition, succession, insolvency, dispute, or regulatory restriction
7. Suspension, wind-down, closure, data transition, record retention, and archive

# Legal Entity, Brand, Operating Unit, and Facility

- The legal entity is the recognized contracting or ownership body where applicable.
- The brand is the public or commercial identity and may change without erasing legal history.
- An operating unit is a governed subdivision with scoped people, services, facilities, finances, or reporting.
- A facility is a physical place and may be owned, leased, licensed, or merely used by the business.
- A merchant or payout account is a financial channel, not the business itself.
- A provider profile represents professional services and credentials, not the entire organizational identity.

# Authority, Governance, and Administration

- Business authority must be grounded in verified organizational relationships, delegated powers, and effective periods.
- Owners, directors, managers, billing administrators, staff, accountants, providers, and platform administrators require distinct scopes.
- No departed employee or contractor should retain access merely because they created records or originally configured the account.
- Sensitive actions such as changing ownership, bank details, payout destination, tax identity, legal name, or primary administrator require heightened review and audit.
- Break-glass platform support access must remain customer-data-minimized, reason-coded, attributable, and reviewable.

# Services, Clients, Agreements, and Delivery

- Service catalogs must distinguish the selling business, delivering business, provider, location, scope, price basis, taxes, cancellation rules, and required agreements.
- A client, payer, horse owner, guardian, rider, and service recipient may be different parties.
- Service completion, customer acceptance, invoice issuance, payment, refund, and dispute are separate events.
- Bundled services, packages, subscriptions, deposits, retainers, credits, and marketplace bookings require explicit rules.
- Business records must retain customer and horse context without granting broad access to unrelated information.

# Workforce and Institutional Knowledge

- Employees, independent contractors, volunteers, interns, owners, and service providers require distinct classifications and relationships.
- The platform may support scheduling, assignments, credential tracking, time records, communication, and performance context without determining legal employment status.
- Business knowledge, templates, procedures, customer history, and operational records must survive staff departure.
- Private personnel, safeguarding, disciplinary, and compensation information require restricted handling.
- Analytics and AI must not create opaque worker rankings or make final hiring, discipline, termination, compensation, or credential decisions.

# Financial Truth and Responsibility

- The business must be able to distinguish revenue, receivables, liabilities, deposits, restricted funds, refunds, credits, taxes, platform fees, commissions, payouts, and reconciliation items.
- Subscription billing, direct service billing, marketplace payments, and third-party processor balances remain architecturally distinct.
- The party selling the service, owing the payment, receiving funds, bearing tax responsibility, and authorizing refunds must be explicit.
- Processor dashboards and bank feeds are evidence sources, not the sole constitutional ledger.
- Financial corrections require traceability and may not erase prior statements, payments, or disputes.
- No AI or automation may independently move money, change bank instructions, waive material debt, or make final credit decisions without governed authority.

# Marketplace and Network Participation

- Marketplace participation is optional and does not create private-data access or a service relationship.
- Credentials, insurance, reviews, response time, availability, pricing, and service area must retain source, verification, expiration, and challenge rights.
- Ranking and recommendation systems must be explainable, fair, resistant to manipulation, and separated from undisclosed pay-to-play influence.
- Private horse, owner, staff, business-performance, and exact-location data may not be used for marketplace discovery without explicit authority.
- Suspension or removal from the marketplace must not automatically suspend the underlying business account unless separately justified.

# Multi-Facility, Multi-Business, and Group Structures

- A business may operate at multiple facilities, and multiple businesses may operate at one facility.
- Parent, subsidiary, affiliate, franchise, joint venture, contractor, and management relationships must be explicit.
- Cross-business access must not be inferred from shared ownership, shared staff, shared email domain, or shared facility.
- Consolidated reporting may not erase entity-level financial, contractual, privacy, or authority boundaries.
- Shared services such as accounting, support, marketing, or administration require delegated scope and audit.

# Change, Succession, Insolvency, and Closure

## Material Change Events

- Ownership or control change
- Legal entity conversion or merger
- Brand sale or licensing
- Facility acquisition or departure
- Bank, processor, tax, or insurance change
- Administrator succession
- Service-line sale or closure
- Insolvency, receivership, bankruptcy, dissolution, or regulatory restriction

## Continuity Requirements

- Preserve customer, horse, provider, staff, financial, contract, claim, and audit history.
- Revalidate authority and revoke obsolete access.
- Protect restricted records and avoid unauthorized transfer to a successor.
- Maintain lawful access to receipts, agreements, exports, disputes, and open obligations.
- Communicate material changes with appropriate notice and acknowledgment.
- Prevent account abandonment, orphaned integrations, stranded payouts, or inaccessible records.

# Business Analytics and AI Limits

- Business analytics may support revenue, retention, utilization, capacity, service quality, customer health, staffing, and operational planning.
- Official KPIs require defined ownership, source lineage, time semantics, corrections, and certification status.
- Cross-tenant benchmarks require aggregation, small-cell protection, and clear comparability limits.
- AI may draft summaries, identify anomalies, explain invoices, suggest scheduling, and surface missing data, but may not make final legal, financial, employment, medical, welfare, or access decisions.
- No metric may become an unsupported public claim about business quality, safety, profitability, or professional competence.

# Lifecycle Success Criteria

- The business remains identifiable across people, brands, facilities, and vendors.
- Authority survives staff turnover without becoming overbroad.
- Services, agreements, work, money, and responsibility remain traceable.
- Multi-business and multi-facility structures preserve separation.
- Succession or closure does not strand records, customers, horses, obligations, or funds.
