# 1. Constitutional Purpose

This document establishes the controlling EquineSync constitutional model for agreements, consent, authorization, signatures, acknowledgments, approvals, notices, amendments, renewals, suspensions, terminations, derived operational effects, and evidentiary continuity.

It exists so EquineSync can reliably answer:

1. What agreement, consent, authorization, disclosure, acknowledgment, approval, or notice exists?
2. Which agreement family, package, template, version, clause set, exhibit, and language controlled?
3. Who were the parties, subjects, beneficiaries, obligors, obligees, signers, and approving actors?
4. In what capacity did each person act?
5. What authority supported each represented action?
6. What exact material was presented to each recipient?
7. What did each person agree to, consent to, authorize, acknowledge, approve, refuse, or withdraw?
8. What conditions had to occur before the instrument became effective?
9. When did each term, permission, obligation, restriction, or benefit become effective?
10. Was the action conditional, partial, purpose-limited, revocable, renewable, time-limited, or subject to countersignature?
11. What authentication, identity, signature, delivery, and authority evidence supports it?
12. What notice was required, delivered, received, failed, waived, or disputed?
13. Has the agreement been negotiated, amended, renewed, suspended, superseded, challenged, rescinded, or terminated?
14. What operational effects may EquineSync derive from it under approved rules?
15. Which rights, obligations, permissions, restrictions, and survival terms remain active?
16. What records must remain preserved after transfer, closure, death, dispute, vendor failure, account termination, or system migration?

This model prevents EquineSync from collapsing distinct legal and operational concepts into one overloaded `signed_document` object.

A signature is not the agreement.  
An agreement is not the rendered PDF.  
A document is not necessarily a contract.  
Consent is not always contractual assent.  
Acknowledgment is not consent.  
Authorization is not ownership.  
Approval is not signature.  
Acceptance is not always execution.  
Execution is not always effectiveness.  
A template is not an agreement instance.  
A clause is not the entire agreement.  
A checkbox is not always sufficient evidence.  
A delivery event is not necessarily legal notice.  
A guardian signature is not the minor's own assent.  
A business representative is not automatically an authorized signatory.  
A signature-provider certificate is evidence of an event, not proof of legal capacity.  
An expired agreement does not erase historical obligations.  
A terminated agreement does not erase prior performance.  
A replacement agreement does not silently rewrite the past.  
A consent withdrawal does not retroactively erase completed lawful actions.  

These distinctions are constitutional.

## 2.1 Consent and Authorization Ownership Boundary

This model owns:

- consent and authorization creation;
- presentation, capacity, granularity, and separation;
- acceptance, signature, acknowledgment, and authority evidence;
- consent receipts;
- withdrawal, revocation, expiration, renewal, and supersession;
- agreement-derived operational-effect records.

The Master Permission and Access-Control Model owns how a valid, active, scoped consent or authorization is enforced in an access decision.

The Master Privacy and Data Protection Model owns whether consent is an appropriate or required basis for personal-data processing and owns privacy rights, transparency, and data-subject requests.

The Master Communication, Notification, and Notice Model owns channel-specific communication consent and notice delivery. The Master Media, Files, and Digital Asset Governance Model owns asset-specific capture, use, licensing, and publication rights.

No lower-order system may create a second consent lifecycle, infer consent from silence, or broaden authorization beyond this model.

Where a lower-order domain document conflicts with this model, this model controls unless a later constitutional amendment expressly states otherwise.

This model does not itself determine:

- legal enforceability in a specific jurisdiction;
- whether a person has legal capacity under local law;
- whether a waiver, release, indemnity, liquidated-damages clause, arbitration clause, or limitation of liability is enforceable;
- whether an electronic signature satisfies a specific statutory requirement;
- whether a party breached an agreement;
- whether a contractual remedy is available;
- whether a court or regulator would enforce a provision;
- whether a clause is appropriate for a particular customer or transaction.

Those questions require jurisdiction-aware legal review. This model supplies the canonical platform structure necessary to preserve and operationalize agreement truth responsibly.

# 4. Constitutional Principles

## 4.1 Agreement truth is distinct from document presentation

An agreement is a governed legal and operational record composed of:

- agreement identity;
- agreement family;
- agreement type;
- parties and non-party subjects;
- capacities;
- authority evidence;
- terms;
- clauses;
- exhibits;
- version;
- presentation package;
- signature and acceptance evidence;
- effective period;
- conditions;
- obligations;
- rights;
- restrictions;
- permissions;
- notices;
- amendments;
- termination and survival rules;
- audit history.

A PDF, image, envelope, or HTML page is a rendering or evidence artifact. It is not the complete canonical agreement model.

## 4.2 Consent, acknowledgment, authorization, approval, and assent are distinct

EquineSync must distinguish:

- contractual assent;
- informed consent;
- operational authorization;
- instruction;
- election;
- policy acknowledgment;
- risk acknowledgment;
- disclosure receipt;
- permission;
- approval;
- attestation;
- certification;
- refusal;
- revocation;
- withdrawal.

No user interface, event, database field, or report may use these concepts interchangeably.

## 4.3 Authority must be evaluated at the moment of action

The system must determine whether the actor possessed sufficient authority when the action occurred.

Authority must not be inferred solely from:

- account ownership;
- organization membership;
- role title;
- email domain;
- payment history;
- horse custody;
- possession of a document;
- receipt of an invitation;
- prior authority;
- relationship status;
- administrative access;
- property ownership;
- business ownership.

## 4.4 Capacity must be explicit

A person may act:

- individually;
- as guardian;
- as parent;
- as authorized representative;
- as organization signatory;
- as horse owner;
- as custodian;
- as barn operator;
- as trainer;
- as payer;
- as guarantor;
- as fiduciary;
- as executor;
- as trustee;
- as agent;
- as attorney-in-fact;
- as service provider;
- as witness;
- as notary where supported;
- as internal approver.

The same person may act in different capacities in different instruments.

## 4.5 Exact version and presentation context must be preserved

The exact material presented to each party must be reconstructable.

The system must preserve:

- template version;
- generated instance version;
- clause versions;
- variables;
- attachments;
- exhibits;
- schedules;
- disclosures;
- incorporated documents;
- language version;
- rendering hash;
- package manifest;
- signature-time version;
- acceptance language;
- presentation method;
- material-change markers.

## 4.6 Agreement status and effect are temporal

Agreement truth changes over time.

The system must preserve:

- draft;
- negotiation;
- approval;
- issuance;
- presentation;
- viewing;
- partial execution;
- full execution;
- effectiveness;
- suspension;
- amendment;
- renewal;
- default;
- cure;
- expiration;
- termination;
- rescission;
- revocation;
- supersession;
- dispute;
- archival.

Historical states must remain queryable.

## 4.7 Execution does not automatically create every possible effect

Even a fully executed instrument may not automatically:

- create legal ownership;
- grant every permission;
- activate a workflow;
- authorize payment changes outside its scope;
- authorize treatment beyond its stated limits;
- override a court order;
- override mandatory law;
- override a higher-order agreement;
- erase prior rights;
- authorize production behavior not approved by governance.

## 4.8 Refusal, withdrawal, revocation, and nonresponse are separate

EquineSync must distinguish when a party:

- refuses;
- declines;
- withdraws consent;
- revokes authorization;
- disputes capacity;
- disputes signature;
- requests correction;
- challenges version;
- fails to respond;
- fails to receive;
- abandons the process;
- allows an invitation to expire.

## 4.9 Agreements survive account and relationship changes

Agreement evidence must remain intact after:

- account closure;
- organization departure;
- horse transfer;
- barn transfer;
- death;
- incapacity;
- business closure;
- identity merge;
- system migration;
- signature-vendor change;
- dispute;
- termination.

## 4.10 External signature and document providers are adapters

External providers may:

- render;
- deliver;
- collect signatures;
- collect initials;
- track recipient activity;
- produce certificates;
- return webhook events;
- store provider copies.

They must not become the canonical authority for:

- party identity;
- capacity;
- authority;
- agreement meaning;
- contract hierarchy;
- operational effect;
- retention;
- dispute resolution;
- canonical status.

## 4.11 Material terms require meaningful presentation

Material terms must not be obscured through:

- hidden links;
- inaccessible attachments;
- preselected optional consent;
- misleading buttons;
- bundled unrelated permissions;
- false urgency;
- confusing negative consent;
- presentation designed to discourage review.

## 4.12 Derived effects must be traceable and reversible where appropriate

Any relationship, permission, obligation, schedule, payment authority, access grant, or workflow effect derived from an agreement must remain traceable to:

- the agreement;
- the controlling clause;
- the effective period;
- the relevant party or subject;
- the activation event;
- the revocation, termination, or supersession rule.

# 6. Agreement Family and Hierarchy Model

## 6.1 Agreement family

An `Agreement Family` groups instruments that collectively govern one relationship or transaction.

Examples:

- master services agreement plus statements of work;
- boarding agreement plus horse-specific schedules;
- lesson agreement plus waiver and minor consent;
- horse lease plus care schedule and insurance exhibit;
- platform terms plus order form and data-processing addendum.

## 6.2 Instrument hierarchy

A family may contain:

1. master agreement;
2. order form;
3. statement of work;
4. rider;
5. schedule;
6. exhibit;
7. addendum;
8. amendment;
9. policy incorporated by reference;
10. disclosure;
11. consent receipt;
12. termination notice.

## 6.3 Order of precedence

Where instruments conflict, the family must preserve an explicit order of precedence.

Order of precedence must not be inferred solely from:

- creation date;
- signature date;
- upload order;
- filename;
- document length.

## 6.4 Incorporation by reference

An incorporated document must be:

- specifically identified;
- versioned;
- available to the recipient;
- included in the package manifest or referenced with stable evidence;
- preserved as presented at execution.

A mutable webpage must not be treated as fixed incorporated terms unless its controlling version is preserved.

## 7.6 Party

A `Party` is a person or organization formally bound, benefited, protected, authorized, or otherwise participating in an agreement.

## 7.7 Non-Party Subject

A `Non-Party Subject` is an entity affected by the instrument without necessarily being a legal party.

Examples:

- a horse;
- a minor participant;
- a facility;
- a payment method holder;
- a service provider;
- an emergency contact;
- an insured animal.

## 7.8 Party Capacity

`Party Capacity` identifies the role in which a person or organization acts.

## 7.9 Signer

A `Signer` is an actor who applies a signature or signature-equivalent event.

A signer may be different from the party represented.

## 7.10 Consenter

A `Consenter` grants consent for a defined purpose and scope.

## 7.11 Authorizer

An `Authorizer` grants operational authority.

## 7.12 Acknowledging Party

An `Acknowledging Party` confirms receipt, awareness, or understanding.

## 7.13 Approver

An `Approver` permits a draft, clause, package, exception, or operational effect to proceed.

## 7.14 Beneficiary

A `Beneficiary` receives a right, benefit, protection, service, or permission under the agreement.

## 7.15 Obligor

An `Obligor` is responsible for performing an obligation.

## 7.16 Obligee

An `Obligee` is entitled to performance of an obligation.

## 7.17 Clause

A `Clause` is a discrete term or provision.

## 7.18 Exhibit, Schedule, Rider, and Addendum

These are separately versioned instruments incorporated into or attached to an agreement family.

## 7.19 Disclosure

A `Disclosure` presents information that may require receipt, acknowledgment, separate assent, or enhanced presentation.

## 7.20 Notice

A `Notice` is a governed communication intended to satisfy an agreement, policy, legal, or operational notice requirement.

## 7.21 Signature Event

A `Signature Event` records an electronic or physical signature action.

## 7.22 Acceptance Event

An `Acceptance Event` records assent without necessarily applying a formal signature.

## 7.23 Consent Event

A `Consent Event` records grant, limitation, refusal, withdrawal, or expiration of consent.

## 7.24 Authorization Event

An `Authorization Event` records grant, limitation, suspension, fulfillment, revocation, or expiration of operational authority.

## 7.25 Acknowledgment Event

An `Acknowledgment Event` records receipt, awareness, or acknowledgment.

## 7.26 Obligation

An `Obligation` is a required act, payment, delivery, restriction, or continuing duty.

## 7.27 Right or Benefit

A `Right or Benefit` is an entitlement created or recognized by an agreement.

## 7.28 Restriction or Prohibition

A `Restriction` limits conduct, access, disclosure, or use.

## 7.29 Condition

A `Condition` is an event that controls effectiveness, performance, continuation, or termination.

## 7.30 Amendment

An `Amendment` changes an existing agreement while preserving the original.

## 7.31 Renewal

A `Renewal` extends or re-establishes effect for a new period.

## 7.32 Termination

A `Termination` ends some or all future effect.

## 7.33 Rescission

`Rescission` is a governed action intended to unwind or cancel an agreement, subject to legal and operational review.

## 7.34 Supersession

`Supersession` identifies that a later instrument replaces some or all of an earlier instrument.

## 7.35 Consent Receipt

A `Consent Receipt` is the canonical evidence package showing what consent was requested, what was presented, what the person chose, and when the choice became effective.

## 7.36 Authority Snapshot

An `Authority Snapshot` preserves the authority evidence and evaluation used at the time of signing or approval.

## 7.37 Operational Effect Ledger

The `Operational Effect Ledger` records every system effect activated, modified, suspended, or revoked because of an agreement.

# 74. Canonical Glossary

## Agreement
A governed record of rights, duties, permissions, restrictions, commitments, conditions, and remedies among identified parties.

## Agreement Family
A set of related instruments that collectively govern one relationship or transaction.

## Agreement Instance
A specific generated agreement presented to identified parties.

## Agreement Package
The complete set of instruments, disclosures, exhibits, and actions presented together.

## Agreement Template
A reusable, version-controlled source used to generate agreement instances.

## Acknowledgment
Confirmation of receipt, awareness, or understanding that does not necessarily constitute consent or contractual assent.

## Amendment
A change to an existing agreement that preserves the original.

## Approval
A decision allowing a draft, clause, exception, package, or action to proceed.

## Authorization
A grant of operational authority for a defined action, subject, purpose, and period.

## Authority Snapshot
The evidence and evaluation supporting a person's authority at a specific time.

## Capacity
The role or legal position in which a person acts.

## Clause
A discrete agreement provision with its own type, version, and effect.

## Condition Precedent
An event that must occur before an agreement or obligation becomes effective.

## Consent
A voluntary grant of permission for a defined purpose and scope.

## Consent Receipt
The canonical evidence package showing what consent was requested, presented, chosen, and made effective.

## Countersignature
A signature by an additional party required to complete execution.

## Disclosure
Information presented to a party, sometimes requiring acknowledgment or separate assent.

## Execution
Completion of required signature or acceptance events.

## Notice
A governed communication intended to satisfy an agreement, legal, policy, or operational notice requirement.

## Obligation
A required act, payment, delivery, restriction, or continuing duty.

## Operational Effect Ledger
The canonical record of system effects activated or changed because of an agreement.

## Party
A person or organization formally participating in an agreement.

## Contractual Representation
A statement of fact made within an agreement. This is distinct from identity representation, in which one actor acts for another principal.

## Rescission
A governed action intended to unwind or cancel an agreement, subject to authorized review.

## Revocation
Withdrawal of previously granted authorization.

## Signature Event
The recorded application of a signature or signature-equivalent action.

## Supersession
Explicit replacement of some or all of an earlier instrument by a later instrument.

## Termination
Ending some or all future effect of an agreement.

## Warranty
A contractual assurance concerning a fact, condition, quality, or performance.

## Withdrawal
A person's removal of consent.
