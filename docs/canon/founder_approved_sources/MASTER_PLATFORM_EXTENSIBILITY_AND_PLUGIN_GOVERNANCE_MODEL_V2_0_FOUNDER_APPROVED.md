# EXECUTIVE CONSTITUTIONAL SUMMARY

EquineSync may support extensibility to increase customer value, interoperability, specialized equine workflows, reporting, automation, and innovation. Extensibility is not a license to create an alternate platform government.

EquineSync shall operate under a **secure-by-default, extensible-by-exception** model. Every extension must remain identifiable, bounded, purpose-limited, least-privileged, monitored, isolated, reviewable, and revocable. Plugins and integrations are subordinate platform participants. They may add capability, but they may not add hidden authority.

No extension may establish or control an alternate:

- identity authority;
- permission system;
- ownership truth;
- financial truth;
- consent authority;
- safeguarding authority;
- horse-welfare authority;
- record-retention authority;
- audit or evidentiary authority;
- encryption or key-management authority;
- or constitutional system of record.

Every plugin, publisher, developer, API consumer, marketplace participant, external processor, automation, agent, integration, and embedded service automatically inherits all applicable current and future EquineSync constitutional governance.

The constitutional promise of this Model is simple:

> **Extensibility may connect systems and enrich workflows. It may not dissolve the boundaries that protect horses, people, records, money, identity, and trust.**

# 3. DEFINITIONS

## 3.1 Extension

Any software, service, application, agent, workflow, component, or processing arrangement that adds to, connects to, modifies, supplements, renders within, or acts through EquineSync.

## 3.2 Plugin

An extension authorized to execute, render, process, communicate, or act through an EquineSync-approved extension mechanism.

## 3.3 Integration

A governed connection between EquineSync and an external system. An integration is treated as a plugin where its functional behavior falls within plugin governance.

## 3.4 Embedded Service

A third-party or separately operated capability presented within an EquineSync-controlled surface or workflow.

## 3.5 Publisher

The identified legal person or organization responsible for a plugin's publication, operation, representations, support, security, and compliance.

## 3.6 Developer

A verified person or authorized team that creates, maintains, tests, or supports an extension.

## 3.7 Plugin Installation

A specific authorized deployment or connection of a plugin to a tenant, organization, facility, user population, environment, or other bounded context.

## 3.8 Plugin Service Identity

A unique non-human identity assigned to a plugin installation, service, or execution context.

## 3.9 Extension Point

A documented and governed interface through which extension behavior may occur.

## 3.10 Protected Constitutional Domain

A platform domain whose truth, authority, security, evidence, or final decision control may not be independently altered or replaced by a plugin.

## 3.11 Derived Data

Information created from EquineSync data, including summaries, scores, classifications, recommendations, embeddings, indexes, profiles, behavioral patterns, predictions, risk indicators, and transformed datasets.

## 3.12 External Processing

Processing performed outside an EquineSync-controlled environment or by an external processor.

## 3.13 Material Change

A change that may affect permissions, data access, processing purpose, external destination, subprocessor use, security posture, AI provider, runtime behavior, user experience, risk classification, commercial terms, ownership, or compliance.

## 3.14 Constitutional Record

A record, event, relationship, claim, authorization, decision, status, or evidence object governed by EquineSync constitutional canon.

## 3.15 Shadow System of Record

An undisclosed, uncontrolled, or conflicting external repository or decision system that is treated as authoritative for an EquineSync constitutional domain.

## 3.16 Permission Laundering

Any pooling, combining, transferring, inferring, or reusing of authority across users, tenants, facilities, horses, roles, installations, sessions, environments, or plugins to produce access broader than any valid authorization permits.

## 3.17 Safety-Critical Extension

An extension whose failure, delay, incorrect output, or unavailability could materially affect horse welfare, human safety, emergency response, medication, feeding, turnout, safeguarding, or another time-sensitive protective function.

## 3.18 Material Plugin Action

An action that reads, creates, changes, exports, transmits, suppresses, recommends, or influences data, rights, money, access, workflows, safety, or authoritative records in a manner requiring evidence and review.

# 4. FOUNDATIONAL CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLES

## 4.1 Secure by Default, Extensible by Exception

No extension capability is presumed safe or permitted merely because it is technically possible. Extensibility must be affirmatively designed, reviewed, authorized, monitored, and revocable.

## 4.2 Extensibility Is a Privilege

Developer, publisher, marketplace, API, and installation access are privileges. EquineSync may condition, restrict, delay, suspend, quarantine, revoke, or withdraw them when necessary to protect constitutional interests.

## 4.3 Plugins Are Subordinate Participants

Plugins do not possess independent constitutional authority. They may act only through approved mechanisms and within a valid authority chain.

## 4.4 Least Privilege

A plugin may receive only the minimum permissions, data, duration, destination, and operational authority necessary for its approved function.

## 4.5 Purpose Limitation

Plugin authority is limited by approved purpose as well as by data category and action. Data accessed for one purpose may not be repurposed without new review and authorization.

## 4.6 Data Minimization

Plugins must not collect data merely because it may be useful later. Requested data must be necessary, proportionate, and tied to the approved function.

## 4.7 Explicit Attribution

Every material action must be attributable to the publisher, plugin, version, installation, environment, service identity, initiating actor, and authority relied upon.

## 4.8 Isolation

Plugin execution, data, credentials, network access, resource use, and failures must be isolated from the platform, other tenants, and other plugins.

## 4.9 Revocability

EquineSync and properly authorized customer administrators must be able to terminate or restrict plugin authority promptly, including queued, cached, event-driven, tokenized, and offline pathways.

## 4.10 Safe Degradation

Plugin failure must not cause uncontrolled platform failure, corrupt constitutional records, conceal incomplete work, or abandon time-sensitive welfare tasks.

## 4.11 Human Authority

Plugins may assist consequential decisions. They may not hold final authority where EquineSync governance requires human, professional, legal, guardian, owner, or organizational decision-making.

## 4.12 No Shadow Systems of Record

Plugins may not create undisclosed or uncontrolled external systems of record for protected constitutional domains.

## 4.13 No Permission Laundering

No plugin may combine or reuse authority to create broader access than any valid grant permits.

## 4.14 Evidentiary Continuity

Plugin activity must preserve provenance, auditability, historical reconstruction, correction, dispute review, and legal-hold obligations.

## 4.15 Provider Neutrality

This Model is independent of any specific cloud, marketplace, sandbox, payment, AI, identity, or infrastructure provider.

## 4.16 Portability and Exit

Plugins must not create unreasonable customer lock-in or destroy access to customer-controlled information when a relationship ends.

## 4.17 Future Constitutional Inheritance

Every extension automatically inherits later EquineSync constitutional requirements. Prior approval does not preserve weaker governance indefinitely.

# 7. PLUGIN RISK CLASSIFICATION

## 7.1 Mandatory Classification

Every plugin must receive a documented risk classification before approval, installation, or production operation.

## 7.2 Class I: Low Risk

Typical characteristics:

- no sensitive data;
- no write authority;
- no external transmission;
- no background processing;
- no safety dependence;
- no financial effect;
- and no persistent customer data.

Examples may include static presentation components, public-data views, and isolated calculators.

## 7.3 Class II: Moderate Risk

Typical characteristics:

- bounded customer-data access;
- limited reporting or workflow assistance;
- low-consequence notifications;
- no protected-domain control;
- and limited or no external processing.

## 7.4 Class III: High Risk

Typical characteristics:

- record modification;
- sensitive personal data;
- equine health or treatment information;
- financial integrations;
- minors' data;
- AI processing;
- offline storage;
- external transmission;
- high-volume exports;
- or operational dependence.

## 7.5 Class IV: Critical or Restricted

Typical characteristics:

- identity-adjacent services;
- authorization-adjacent services;
- settlement or payment authority;
- safeguarding;
- ownership transfer;
- legal evidence;
- emergency horse-welfare workflows;
- cryptographic authority;
- or privileged administration.

Class IV functionality shall ordinarily remain EquineSync-controlled. Third-party operation may be prohibited regardless of proposed safeguards.

## 7.6 Classification Factors

Classification must consider:

- data sensitivity and volume;
- read, write, delete, or export capability;
- external destinations;
- AI or automated agency;
- offline storage;
- affected populations, including minors;
- horse-welfare and human-safety impact;
- financial or legal consequence;
- operational dependency;
- reversibility;
- number of tenants;
- scope of permissions;
- processor and subprocessor chain;
- execution privilege;
- and likelihood and severity of failure.

## 7.7 Reclassification

EquineSync may reclassify a plugin after a material change, incident, vulnerability, usage expansion, regulatory change, publisher change, constitutional amendment, or new evidence.

## 7.8 Minimum Control Rule

A risk class establishes a minimum control floor. EquineSync may impose stronger controls based on actual circumstances.

# 9. PLUGIN IDENTITY, CREDENTIALS, AND DELEGATED AUTHORITY

## 9.1 Independent Service Identity

Every plugin installation must use a unique non-human service identity. A plugin may not operate solely through a shared human credential.

## 9.2 Identity Separation

Plugin identities must remain distinguishable from:

- human users;
- tenant administrators;
- EquineSync system services;
- other plugins;
- publisher staff;
- and test identities.

## 9.3 Credential Requirements

Credentials must be:

- independently issued;
- least privileged;
- environment-specific;
- securely stored;
- attributable;
- monitored;
- rotatable;
- and immediately revocable where necessary.

## 9.4 Credential Prohibitions

Credentials must not be:

- embedded in public client code;
- stored in plaintext;
- shared across unrelated tenants;
- reused across production and non-production;
- transferred between plugins;
- or exposed to publisher personnel beyond the minimum necessary access.

## 9.5 Delegated Authority Chain

Every material action must preserve the chain between:

- the initiating human or system actor;
- the plugin;
- the plugin installation;
- the plugin version;
- the service identity;
- the publisher;
- the tenant and environment;
- the permission grant;
- and any delegated relationship authority.

## 9.6 Human Credential Impersonation

Plugins must not impersonate human users or conceal automated action as human action.

## 9.7 Revocation Scope

EquineSync must be able to revoke:

- publisher access;
- developer access;
- plugin service identities;
- installations;
- tokens;
- certificates;
- signing keys;
- webhook secrets;
- event subscriptions;
- and environment credentials.

## 9.8 Production Isolation

Production credentials may not be used in development, testing, demonstration, sandbox, or staging environments.

# 10. PERMISSION GOVERNANCE

## 10.1 Granular Least Privilege

Permissions must be limited by all relevant dimensions, including:

- tenant;
- organization;
- facility;
- horse;
- record category;
- role;
- relationship;
- actor;
- action;
- purpose;
- workflow;
- time;
- environment;
- and data classification.

## 10.2 Broad Permissions

Broad or unrestricted administrative permissions are prohibited by default. Any exception requires a defined need, heightened review, explicit authorization, continuous monitoring, and periodic reassessment.

## 10.3 No Permission Laundering

A plugin may not combine, pool, infer, transfer, or reuse permissions across:

- users;
- tenants;
- facilities;
- organizations;
- horses;
- installations;
- sessions;
- environments;
- or plugins.

Authority granted in one context may not satisfy an authorization requirement in another.

## 10.4 No Plugin-to-Plugin Permission Inheritance

One plugin may not inherit, borrow, or depend on another plugin's permissions. Any mediated inter-plugin workflow must independently validate authority.

## 10.5 Permission Expiration

Temporary, delegated, elevated, pilot, trial, and incident-specific permissions must expire. Expiration must invalidate, as applicable:

- access tokens;
- refresh tokens;
- cached credentials;
- queued jobs;
- scheduled automation;
- offline authority;
- background processes;
- and event subscriptions.

## 10.6 Immediate Revocation

EquineSync and properly authorized administrators must be able to revoke plugin authority promptly. Revocation must address logical authorization, token invalidation, queued work, events, offline operations, external processor access, and residual data.

## 10.7 Permission Renewal

Material permission expansion requires renewed review and authorization. Silent permission expansion through an update is prohibited.

## 10.8 Relationship-Scoped Authority

Where access depends on ownership, guardianship, facility membership, provider relationship, delegated care, employment, or another relationship, the plugin must inherit the narrowest valid relationship scope.

## 10.9 Shared Care and Delegated Assistance

Where EquineSync permits horse-specific, task-limited, time-limited delegated assistance, plugins may expose only the minimum horse and task information necessary to perform the authorized activity. Such access does not create general record access, ownership authority, continuing access, or unrelated private-data access.

# 11. PURPOSE-BOUND AUTHORIZATION AND CONSENT

## 11.1 Purpose Limitation

Plugin access must be limited by the approved purpose for which authority was granted.

## 11.2 Secondary Use Prohibition

Data accessed for one purpose must not be reused for:

- advertising;
- unrelated analytics;
- product development;
- benchmarking;
- resale;
- lead generation;
- profiling;
- cross-customer comparison;
- general model training;
- or other secondary use

unless separately authorized and constitutionally permitted.

## 11.3 Purpose Change

A purpose change is a material change and requires renewed review and authorization.

## 11.4 Consent Quality

Where consent is the applicable basis, it must be informed, specific, separable from unrelated terms, capable of withdrawal, and obtained from the proper rights-holder.

## 11.5 Consent Is Not a Cure-All

Consent does not authorize prohibited processing, eliminate another person's rights, waive safeguarding duties, or validate an otherwise unlawful or constitutionally impermissible disclosure.

## 11.6 Organizational Authorization

An administrator's approval does not automatically waive the rights of horse owners, guardians, employees, providers, boarders, riders, or other affected persons. Additional authorization may be required.

## 11.7 Withdrawal

Withdrawal of consent or authority must propagate to plugin access, tokens, background processing, event subscriptions, offline data, and external processors as required.

# 12. DATA ACCESS, MODIFICATION, EXPORT, AND DELETION

## 12.1 Read Access

Plugins may read only the minimum data necessary for approved functionality and only within the originating authority chain.

## 12.2 Write Access

Plugins may modify records only through approved EquineSync services that enforce:

- authority;
- validation;
- provenance;
- data integrity;
- conflict handling;
- required human review;
- and auditability.

## 12.3 No Direct Constitutional Record Mutation

Plugins may not directly modify protected constitutional records or bypass platform validation.

## 12.4 Deletion

Plugins may not directly and permanently delete constitutional records. Deletion, suppression, erasure, and disposition must occur through governed EquineSync services consistent with retention, privacy, claims, evidence, and legal-hold requirements.

## 12.5 Historical Access

Historical access remains subject to current authorization, relationship status, record stewardship, transfer rules, retention, privacy, legal hold, and evidentiary continuity.

A plugin does not acquire historical entitlement broader than the actor possesses.

## 12.6 Export

Plugin-facilitated export requires:

- proper authority;
- defined purpose;
- approved scope;
- secure delivery;
- provenance;
- classification;
- and audit evidence.

## 12.7 External Transmission

External transmission is permitted only to declared, approved, and authorized destinations. Undeclared routing, shadow replication, silent secondary disclosure, and hidden enrichment are prohibited.

## 12.8 Data Residency

Plugins must not violate applicable tenant, contractual, jurisdictional, regulatory, or user-specific data-residency requirements.

## 12.9 Data-Subject and User Rights

Plugins may not interfere with access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, portability, consent withdrawal, or other applicable rights.

## 12.10 No Data Hoarding

Plugins must not retain data longer than necessary for the approved purpose or applicable lawful obligation.

# 13. DERIVED DATA, INFERENCES, AND ANALYTICAL OUTPUTS

## 13.1 Derived Data Remains Governed

Data derived from EquineSync records remains subject to governance based on source sensitivity, identifiability, purpose, effect, and constitutional significance.

## 13.2 Covered Derived Data

Derived data includes:

- summaries;
- predictions;
- recommendations;
- rankings;
- risk indicators;
- embeddings;
- indexes;
- classifications;
- behavioral patterns;
- profiles;
- scores;
- and transformed datasets.

## 13.3 No Unrestricted Ownership Claim

A publisher may not claim unrestricted ownership over derived data merely because the publisher generated the transformation or inference.

## 13.4 Use and Retention Limits

Derived data may not be retained, sold, transferred, cross-linked, used for general model training, or repurposed beyond the authority applicable to the source data.

## 13.5 Inference Sensitivity

An inference may be as sensitive as, or more sensitive than, the source data. Risk classification must consider what the output reveals, not only what the input contained.

## 13.6 De-Identification

De-identification claims must be verified. A plugin may not treat data as unrestricted where re-identification, singling out, or sensitive inference remains reasonably possible.

## 13.7 No Cross-Tenant Learning

Identifiable or tenant-linkable derived data must not be used to improve outputs for another tenant unless a separately approved, constitutionally compliant, non-identifiable process expressly permits it.

Undisclosed subprocessors are prohibited.

## 14.6 Provider Exit

Publishers must maintain reasonable exit, replacement, and continuity plans for material providers.

## 14.7 Cross-Border Processing

Cross-border transfer must be lawful, disclosed where required, consistent with data-residency commitments, and supported by appropriate safeguards.

# 15. SECURITY ARCHITECTURE AND RUNTIME ISOLATION

## 15.1 Mandatory Isolation

Executable plugins must operate within approved isolation boundaries. Isolation must address code execution, memory, storage, network access, credentials, events, queues, and tenant data.

## 15.2 Arbitrary Code Execution

Unrestricted arbitrary code execution is prohibited. Extension execution must occur through controlled runtimes, approved interfaces, or provider-isolated services.

## 15.3 Filesystem Access

Plugins may not directly access host or customer filesystems. Any storage access must use scoped, isolated, encrypted, and governed platform services.

## 15.4 Network Access

Inbound and outbound network access must be:

- declared;
- authenticated;
- integrity-protected;
- allow-listed or otherwise tightly controlled;
- monitored;
- rate limited;
- and limited to approved destinations and purposes.

## 15.5 Resource Controls

Risk-appropriate controls must include:

- CPU limits;
- memory limits;
- storage limits;
- execution timeouts;
- concurrency limits;
- queue limits;
- recursion limits;
- retry limits;
- backpressure;
- and termination controls.

## 15.6 Failure Isolation

Plugin failure must not:

- crash the platform;
- corrupt platform state;
- deadlock shared services;
- expose another tenant's data;
- compromise another plugin;
- impair audit evidence;
- or silently strand critical workflows.

## 15.7 Inter-Plugin Isolation

Plugins may not communicate directly unless communication occurs through an approved EquineSync mediation layer. Each mediated interaction must independently validate permissions and purpose.

## 15.8 No Host Trust

Plugins must not depend on implicit trust in private APIs, undocumented behavior, internal network position, or platform implementation details.

## 15.9 Secret Handling

Plugin secrets, tokens, certificates, webhook secrets, signing keys, and encryption materials must be stored and used through approved secret-management controls.

## 15.10 Security Boundary Testing

EquineSync may require technical testing that demonstrates tenant isolation, privilege separation, network restrictions, failure containment, and resistance to common abuse paths.

# 16. SECURE DEVELOPMENT AND SOFTWARE SUPPLY CHAIN

## 16.1 Code Signing

Every executable or distributable plugin package must be digitally signed by an approved publisher using EquineSync-approved signing and verification controls. Unsigned or unverifiable packages may not execute.

## 16.2 Integrity Verification

Integrity must be verified at installation and update. Higher-risk plugins may require runtime attestation or equivalent continuing integrity evidence.

## 16.3 Software Bill of Materials

Publishers must provide a current Software Bill of Materials or equivalent dependency inventory appropriate to the plugin's architecture and risk.

## 16.4 Dependency Monitoring

Publishers must monitor dependencies for:

- known vulnerabilities;
- malicious or compromised packages;
- abandoned maintenance;
- compromised maintainers;
- prohibited telemetry;
- license conflicts;
- unauthorized network behavior;
- and integrity failures.

## 16.5 Secure Development Lifecycle

Risk-appropriate approval may require evidence of:

- threat modeling;
- abuse-case analysis;
- peer code review;
- static analysis;
- dynamic testing;
- dependency scanning;
- secret scanning;
- penetration testing;
- privacy review;
- accessibility review;
- and remediation verification.

## 16.6 Publisher Assertions Are Not Always Sufficient

EquineSync may require independent or reproducible evidence where the risk, data sensitivity, operational dependency, or history warrants it.

## 16.7 Vulnerability Disclosure

Publishers must maintain a coordinated vulnerability disclosure and remediation process. Material vulnerabilities must be reported promptly to EquineSync.

## 16.8 Critical Dependencies

Critical dependencies must have a remediation, isolation, replacement, or exit plan.

## 16.9 Build and Release Provenance

Material releases must be traceable to approved source, authorized build systems, reviewed changes, and signed release artifacts.

## 16.10 Emergency Security Updates

Expedited processing is permitted only for critical security, safety, resilience, or legal updates. Expedited review does not eliminate evidence, rollback, attribution, or post-release review.

# 17. ENVIRONMENT SEPARATION

## 17.1 Separate Authority

Development, test, sandbox, staging, pilot, and production environments require separate credentials, data access, approval, and operational authority.

## 17.2 No Automatic Promotion

Approval in one environment does not authorize another. Sandbox approval does not create production authority.

## 17.3 Production Data

Production data must not be copied to non-production environments except through an approved, minimized, protected, and auditable process.

## 17.4 Test Data

Testing should use synthetic, masked, or otherwise appropriately protected data whenever reasonably possible.

## 17.5 Demonstrations

Demonstration environments must not expose live customer data or create misleading claims about production readiness.

## 17.6 Pilot Controls

Pilots must have bounded participants, defined duration, monitoring, rollback, customer disclosure, and a clear statement that pilot participation does not equal general availability.

# 18. API GOVERNANCE

## 18.1 Public APIs

Only APIs expressly designated, documented, versioned, secured, and approved for external use may be treated as public APIs.

## 18.2 Private APIs

Internal, constitutional, administrative, migration, security, evidence, and platform-control APIs remain private unless formally elevated through governance.

## 18.3 Versioning

Public APIs must use a documented versioning model addressing behavior, schemas, events, security expectations, and compatibility.

## 18.4 Compatibility

EquineSync should preserve backward compatibility where reasonably possible and safe. Compatibility is not guaranteed for:

- undocumented behavior;
- private APIs;
- deprecated fields;
- insecure behavior;
- unsupported scraping;
- security defects;
- or behavior conflicting with later constitutional requirements.

## 18.5 Deprecation

The standard target for material API deprecation notice is at least twelve months. Shorter notice may be used where required for security, privacy, law, safeguarding, horse welfare, severe reliability risk, or constitutional conflict.

## 18.6 Breaking Changes

Breaking changes require:

- classification;
- impact analysis;
- migration guidance;
- version separation;
- notice;
- testing;
- rollback planning;
- and controlled release authority.

## 18.7 Rate and Abuse Controls

APIs must support risk-appropriate rate limiting, quota controls, anomaly detection, and abuse response.

## 18.8 No Scraping as Substitute

Publishers may not use scraping, browser automation, or undocumented endpoints to bypass unavailable or restricted APIs.

## 18.9 API Credentials

API credentials must be scoped, environment-specific, monitored, and revocable.

# 19. EVENT, WEBHOOK, AND MESSAGE GOVERNANCE

## 19.1 Approved Events Only

Plugins may subscribe only to approved events within authorized scope.

## 19.2 Event Publication

Plugins may publish only approved extension events through governed channels. Plugins may not impersonate native constitutional events.

## 19.3 Delivery Guarantees

Ordering, duplication, latency, consistency, replay, and delivery guarantees apply only where documented.

## 19.4 Replay

Event replay remains subject to authorization, retention, privacy, classification, and evidentiary requirements.

## 19.5 Webhook Security

Webhooks must be authenticated, integrity-protected, replay-resistant, destination-controlled, and revocable.

## 19.6 Event Abuse

Event flooding, recursion loops, uncontrolled fan-out, hidden retries, and unbounded replay are prohibited.

## 19.7 Event Data Minimization

Event payloads must contain only the data necessary for the event's approved purpose. Where feasible, subscribers should retrieve authorized detail through controlled APIs rather than receive unnecessary sensitive data in event payloads.

## 19.8 Delivery Failure

Failed delivery must not silently erase or falsely complete a consequential workflow. Retry, dead-letter, escalation, and reconciliation behavior must be documented where material.

# 20. WORKFLOW, AUTOMATION, AND AGENT GOVERNANCE

## 20.1 Designated Extension Points

Plugins may add optional workflow steps only through designated and governed extension points.

## 20.2 Native Workflow Protection

Plugins may not replace, bypass, shadow, or silently override constitutional workflows.

## 20.3 Blocking Authority

Plugins may not permanently block constitutional operations. Temporary holds must be expressly authorized, visible, time-bounded, attributable, reviewable, and safely bypassable under governed conditions.

## 20.4 Approval Requests

Plugins may request additional approvals through approved mechanisms. They may not independently create binding legal, financial, ownership, medical, welfare, safeguarding, consent, or access authority.

## 20.5 Automated Action Scope

Plugins, bots, agents, and automated services may act only within expressly authorized scopes and thresholds.

## 20.6 Prohibited Autonomous Decisions

Automation must not independently:

- transfer ownership;
- establish or terminate legal rights;
- waive required rights or notices;
- approve material financial transactions;
- change safeguarding status;
- determine medical diagnosis;
- make final welfare determinations;
- expand access rights;
- or eliminate required human approval.

## 20.7 Human Review

Material automated actions must be attributable, reviewable, explainable where applicable, reversible where appropriate, and subject to human authority.

## 20.8 Tool-Using Agents

An agent's tool access must not exceed the narrowest permissions of the initiating actor, plugin, installation, and approved purpose. Agents must not discover or chain tools to create unapproved authority.

## 20.9 Automation Transparency

Users must be able to identify when an action was automated and, where material, what rule, trigger, recommendation, or approval caused it.

## 20.10 Automation Failure

Failed or partial automation must not be presented as successful completion. Material failures require visible state, retry governance, and reconciliation.

# 22. AI PLUGIN AND AGENT GOVERNANCE

## 22.1 Full AI Governance Inheritance

All AI-enabled plugins inherit the EquineSync AI Governance Model without exception.

## 22.2 Assistive Role

AI functionality remains assistive and may not hold final authority over consequential legal, financial, medical, welfare, safeguarding, ownership, consent, or access decisions.

## 22.3 Minimum Necessary Context

AI plugins may receive only the minimum necessary authorized data for the specific request.

## 22.4 Training Restrictions

Identifiable customer operational data, including financial and health-related data, must not be used to train general or shared models.

## 22.5 Output Requirements

AI outputs must:

- be identified as AI-assisted;
- distinguish fact from inference;
- disclose material uncertainty;
- identify sources or provenance where applicable;
- avoid presenting recommendations as determinative authority;
- and preserve meaningful human review.

## 22.6 Provider Requirements

AI providers must satisfy EquineSync requirements concerning privacy, security, provider training, retention, deletion, regional processing, incident response, contractual control, monitoring, and vendor exit.

## 22.7 Prompt and Tool Security

AI plugins must address prompt injection, data exfiltration, untrusted content, tool abuse, hidden instructions, and cross-user context contamination.

## 22.8 No Permission Expansion Through Retrieval

Search, retrieval, embeddings, or model context must not expose data beyond the user's and plugin's authorized scope.

## 22.9 No Silent Canonicalization

AI output is not automatically canonical. A plugin may not silently convert generated output into authoritative records without the required review, validation, and user action.

## 22.10 Insufficient Evidence

AI plugins must refuse, defer, or request additional information where evidence is insufficient for a reliable answer or action.

# 24. HORSE-WELFARE AND HUMAN-SAFETY GOVERNANCE

## 24.1 Safety-Critical Dependence

A plugin may not become the sole authoritative mechanism for a time-sensitive horse-welfare or human-safety action unless separately approved as a safety-critical system.

## 24.2 Required Safeguards

Safety-relevant workflows may require:

- visible human confirmation;
- escalation;
- fallback procedures;
- missed-action detection;
- emergency contact access;
- safe degradation;
- and continuity when the plugin is unavailable.

## 24.3 Professional Judgment

Plugins may not replace veterinary judgment, emergency services, responsible human supervision, direct welfare assessment, or legally required professional authority.

## 24.4 Critical Task Visibility

Medication, feeding, turnout, emergency, and other welfare-critical tasks must not disappear merely because a plugin is disabled, unavailable, or removed.

## 24.5 Safety Notifications

A plugin must not represent that a safety-critical notice was received, acknowledged, or completed without supporting evidence.

## 24.6 Operational Continuity

High-dependence plugins may be required to support export, fallback views, read-only continuity, emergency access, or migration procedures.

## 24.7 No Convenience Override

Convenience, automation, or commercial value may not justify weakening horse-welfare or human-safety protections.

Paid and free plugins may be permitted, subject to commercial policy, consumer protections, financial governance, and transparent terms.

Violations may result in:

- warning;
- corrective action;
- remediation plan;
- increased monitoring;
- installation restriction;
- update freeze;
- quarantine;
- suspension;
- credential revocation;
- delisting;
- removal;
- termination;
- customer notice;
- regulatory reporting;
- contractual remedies;
- or legal action.

## 36.2 Proportionality

Enforcement should be proportionate to risk, intent, impact, recurrence, cooperation, and remediation. Immediate action is permitted where necessary to protect horses, people, data, the platform, or legal obligations.

## 36.3 No Grandfathered Noncompliance

A previously approved plugin may be restricted or removed when later evidence or governance shows unacceptable risk.

## 36.4 Publisher Appeal

Where appropriate and safe, publishers may receive a documented review or appeal path. Appeal does not automatically stay emergency protective action.

## 36.5 Customer Protection

Enforcement actions must consider customer continuity, data return, critical-task access, and safe migration.

# 38. CONSTITUTIONAL INHERITANCE AND CONFLICT RULES

## 38.1 Automatic Inheritance

Every plugin, publisher, developer, marketplace participant, API consumer, integration, processor, and extension automatically inherits all applicable current and future EquineSync constitutional governance.

## 38.2 No Legacy Safe Harbor

A plugin may not continue operating under an older, weaker standard merely because it was approved before a later constitutional rule.

## 38.3 Specific and Protective Rules

Where multiple canons apply:

1. the more specific rule governs over a general rule;
2. the more protective rule governs where both remain applicable;
3. constitutional authority governs over implementation guidance;
4. and unresolved conflicts require formal reconciliation.

## 38.4 No Silent Override

No contract, configuration, technical behavior, marketplace term, or customer preference may silently override constitutional requirements.

# 39. GOVERNANCE GATES

A plugin may not progress to production without passing all applicable gates.

## Gate 1: Functional Classification

The capability is classified by function, category, scope, and protected-domain proximity.

## Gate 2: Risk Classification

The plugin receives a documented risk class and minimum control profile.

## Gate 3: Publisher Verification

The publisher, responsible persons, ownership, security contacts, and legal eligibility are verified.

## Gate 4: Architecture and Data-Flow Review

Architecture, extension points, data categories, destinations, subprocessors, retention, and credentials are documented.

## Gate 5: Identity and Permission Review

Service identity, delegated authority, permission scope, expiration, and revocation are approved.

## Gate 6: Security and Supply-Chain Review

Runtime isolation, secure development, dependencies, signing, vulnerability management, and monitoring satisfy the risk profile.

## Gate 7: Privacy, Purpose, and Consent Review

Data minimization, processing purposes, consent or other authority, residency, rights, and deletion are approved.

## Gate 8: Constitutional Review

The plugin is checked against protected domains and all controlling canon.

## Gate 9: Operational and Welfare Review

Reliability, safe degradation, continuity, incident response, rollback, and any welfare or safety impact are approved.

## Gate 10: Customer Disclosure and Commercial Review

Installation disclosures, pricing, support, marketplace claims, and affected-rights-holder requirements are approved.

## Gate 11: Environment Authorization

The plugin receives approval for a specific environment, scope, and version.

## Gate 12: Production Authorization

A separate authorized decision permits production use.

Passing an earlier gate does not imply passing a later gate.

## 40.1 Written and Bounded

Exceptions must be documented, time-limited, attributable, risk-assessed, monitored, and revocable.

## 40.2 Required Content

- the rule affected;
- the reason;
- scope;
- duration;
- risk;
- compensating controls;
- approving authority;
- monitoring;
- and expiration or review date.

## 40.3 Non-Waivable Protections

No exception may permit:

- cross-tenant access;
- unidentified publishing;
- unlogged constitutional action;
- direct alteration of protected evidence;
- unrestricted arbitrary code execution;
- permission laundering;
- or permanent bypass of constitutional controls.

## 40.4 Emergency Exceptions

Emergency processing may accelerate review but may not eliminate evidence, rollback, attribution, containment, or post-event review.
