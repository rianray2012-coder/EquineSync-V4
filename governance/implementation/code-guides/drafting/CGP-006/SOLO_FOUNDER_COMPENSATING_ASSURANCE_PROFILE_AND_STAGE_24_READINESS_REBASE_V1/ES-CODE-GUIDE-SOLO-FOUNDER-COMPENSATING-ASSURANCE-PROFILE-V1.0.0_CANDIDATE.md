## 1. Purpose And Scope

This candidate profile defines how EquineSync may prepare compensating assurance evidence while it remains a solo-Founder project. It applies only to documentary review and proposed Stage 24 readiness assessment for `ES-CG-00`, `ES-CG-01`, `ES-CG-10`, and `ES-CG-13`.

## 2. Relationship To Program Plan V1.1

Program Plan V1.1 remains controlling. This profile does not revise the Stage 0 through Stage 24 lifecycle, does not bypass exact-byte controls, and does not create activation or implementation authority.

## 3. Relationship To The Founder Determination

The profile implements the custody-complete Founder determination as a candidate assurance model. The determination controls the reclassification of unavailable independent review from an automatic absolute blocker into a disclosed assurance limitation, subject to objective gates and Founder residual-risk decision.

## 4. Applicable Project Conditions

EquineSync is a solo-Founder project with no employees, no retained independent technical reviewer, no outside domain consultant, and no paid assurance budget presently available. Those facts must remain visible in every downstream review or public claim.

## 5. Required Exact-Byte Controls

All reviews must identify the exact guide version, SHA-256, byte length, source path, and source-freeze record. Changed guide bytes require a reopened review against the new bytes.

## 6. Conflict And Non-Independence Disclosure

The Founder owns and directs the project. Founder-led review and Codex-assisted analysis are not independent. They may support a Founder decision only when paired with exact-byte traceability, objective validators, negative tests, and residual-risk disclosure.

## 7. Temporal Separation

Authoring, review, finding recording, remediation, and rereview should be distinct phases. The separation reduces review bias but does not create independence.

## 8. Founder Domain-Owner Review

A structured Founder Domain-Owner Review must evaluate guide practicality for barn, owner, rider, guardian, trainer, lesson, safety, communication, and care-evidence workflows. Completion requires a recorded row-level review and disclosed non-independence.

## 9. Founder Technical Governance Review

A temporally separated Founder Technical Governance Review may assess authority fidelity, implementability, terminology, testability, exception handling, recovery, and cross-guide conflict. It must be described as disclosed self-review, not independent peer review.

## 10. Isolated Machine-Assisted Review Passes

Machine-assisted review passes must be scoped separately across authority, architecture, security, testability, recovery, implementer usability, cross-guide reconciliation, and adversarial misuse. Their output is not independent human review.

## 11. Clean-Room Implementer-Usability Review

The usability review must identify work sequence, component assumptions, test plan, evidence plan, completion checklist, and every material guess. Any material policy invention is a finding.

## 12. Objective Verification Requirements

Future implementation packages must include deterministic validators, unit tests, integration/API tests where applicable, negative tests, authorization and tenant-isolation tests, guardian/minor protection tests, duplicate-effect tests, rollback evidence, and evidence retention controls.

## 13. Threat-Modeling Requirements

High-risk areas include identity, tenancy, facility isolation, support access, guardians and minors, horse care records, payments, uploads, offline behavior, integrations, AI, and administrative misuse. Each threat must record prevention, detection, recovery, verification, and residual-risk treatment.

## 14. Finding Classification

Findings are classified by severity, evidence dependency, lifecycle stage, blocker effect, and closure authority. Open P0 or P1 findings block readiness recommendations. P2/P3 findings may be non-blocking only with express treatment and retained visibility.

## 15. Residual-Risk Treatment

Residual risks remain pending until the Founder affirmatively accepts, modifies, rejects, or defers them. Silence is not acceptance.

## 16. Implementation-Stage Evidence Requirements

Implementation evidence is required after authorized implementation exists. It cannot be claimed before implementation and cannot be used as a circular reason that implementation may never be authorized.

## 17. Runtime-Stage Evidence Requirements

Runtime evidence is required after authorized staging, pilot, or operation exists. It cannot be claimed before runtime use and cannot be used as a circular reason that runtime evidence may never be generated.

## 18. Review Reopening Triggers

Reopen review for changed guide bytes, changed authority, new P0/P1 findings, new security or safeguarding risk, failed validator reliability, unresolved tenant isolation, or any claim that exceeds the recorded evidence.

## 19. Suspension Triggers

Suspend proposed use if a guide byte mismatch appears, a mandatory validator fails, an activation scope is exceeded, tenant isolation is weakened, guardian/minor protection is ambiguous, evidence is fabricated, or rollback cannot be executed.

## 20. Profile Amendment And Retirement Rules

This candidate profile may be revised only by an authorized later workstream. Adoption, accession, custody, amendment, or retirement requires separate Founder disposition and protected repository controls.

## 21. Continuing Disclosure Language

Every use of this profile must disclose the solo-Founder context, non-independence, absence of third-party certification, objective gates, retained conditions, and pending Founder residual-risk decisions.

## 22. Prohibited Claims

The profile prohibits claims of independent audit, third-party certification, veterinary certification, legal advice, cybersecurity certification, accounting certification, runtime assurance, implementation evidence, pilot evidence, production readiness, or guide activation unless separately and truthfully authorized.
