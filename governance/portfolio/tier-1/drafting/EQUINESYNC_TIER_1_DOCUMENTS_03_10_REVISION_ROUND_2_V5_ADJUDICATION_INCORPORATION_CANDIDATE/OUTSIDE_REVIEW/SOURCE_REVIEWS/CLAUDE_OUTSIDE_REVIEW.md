## Executive Determination

This is the Round 2 content. Every headline figure in the review kit reconciles: 96 requirement rows, 2,961 source-disposition rows, 9 workstream PR rows, 19 templates, 5 Founder decisions, 13 lifecycle states across a complete 169-row transition matrix. The package is internally consistent, cryptographically intact, and its validator is honest — I re-ran it independently and got the same 212 checks and the same PASS.

It is also **not the build the review kit points to**, and the gap is not cosmetic: this is a *later* build that incorporates an independent external standards review and claims to have remediated its blocking minimum. The kit's context file describes a predecessor. That is the first finding, and it is structural rather than substantive — but it means no reviewer working from the kit is reviewing what the kit describes.

On the merits, the package is considerably better than the average governance artifact of this kind. The anti-overclaim discipline in Document 03 is real and enforced: all 96 rows are pinned to `SOURCE_TEXT_CANDIDATE` with `verification_method=NOT_PERFORMED`, `verified_coverage=0.0`, and `accepted_normative_requirements=0`, and the coverage table carries an explicit per-row limitation stating that candidate match rate is not requirement coverage. Document 10's nineteen templates are genuinely bounded — `bounded_complete_allowed=NO_IF_EXCLUSIONS_OR_OPEN_ITEMS_REMAIN` on all nineteen, with Prohibited Conclusions sections and example rows marked as non-evidence. Document 07 invents nobody: fourteen functions, fourteen vacancies, `appointment_evidence=NOT_RECORDED` throughout. The V1 "certificate/certification" terminology has been correctly retired in favour of first-party self-declaration language. Credit where due.

The problems are concentrated in three places, and two of them are blocking.

**First, two of the five Founder decisions cannot be answered as written.** `FD-T1R2-001` asks the Founder to approve an "thirteen-state lifecycle vocabulary." No such vocabulary exists anywhere in the package — the shared standard enumerates thirteen states, the transition matrix contains exactly thirteen, and the validator itself hard-asserts `len(states) != 13`. The wrong number propagates verbatim across five artifacts and originates in the external review's own recommended wording, which was adopted without reconciliation against the register it names. `FD-T1R2-004` asks the Founder to accept, remediate, or defer "each open item" in Document 06 — but Document 06 contains zero rows classified as risk, exception, or waiver. All nine rows are findings, all already marked `REMEDIATED_PENDING_FOUNDER_REVIEW`, with every waiver-control column set to `NOT_APPLICABLE`. The decision is scoped to an empty population.

**Second, the findings register of record is incomplete, and closure was self-performed.** The external review raised 28 findings. Document 06 — the instrument `FD-T1R2-004` points at — contains 9 of them. Nineteen are missing, including seven at S2. Of the 28, the producing party marked 25 as `founder_review_needed=NO_FOR_DOCUMENTARY_REMEDIATION`, and all nine Document 06 rows carry `second_reviewer_not_assigned=YES` with identical boilerplate closure evidence. An independent reviewer's findings were closed by the reviewed party, without a second reviewer, and largely routed away from Founder visibility.

**Third, several controls are asserted rather than exercised.** The eight per-document validators are 344-byte existence checks — `validate_document_03_rr2.py` verifies only that the directory contains at least one `.csv`, `.json`, or `.md` file, and the paired test asserts nothing but exit code 0. These are claimed in the delta report as remediation of the external review's blocking minimum. The twelve invalid-state rules are marked `ENFORCED_BY_VALIDATOR` / `failure_capable=YES`, and the validator does implement them — but every one of the eight lifecycle rows holds a single constant value across all twelve dimensions, so no rule can fire on this data and no negative fixture exists. The check that guards this (`invalid_rules_marked_enforced`) verifies only that the CSV *says* the rules are enforced.

None of this requires rebuilding the package. The blocking items are narrow: a wrong number in five files, and a decision frame pointed at the wrong register. **Disposition: `REVISION_REQUIRED`.**

`NOT_ADOPTED`; `NOT_ACTIVE`; `IMPLEMENTATION_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; `PRODUCTION_USE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; `MERGE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; `CERTIFICATION_NOT_COMPLETE`; `FOUNDER_REVIEW_REQUIRED`; `UNRESOLVED_ITEMS_REMAIN_OPEN_AS_IDENTIFIED`.

## Document Assessments

### Document 03 — Requirements traceability (96 rows, 57 columns)
**Strongest anti-overclaim discipline in the package, weakest underlying corpus.** Evidence classes are cleanly separated across distinct columns — documentary (`authority_state`, `source_locator`), code (`implementation_path`, `path_verification_state`, `verification_commit`), test (`test_id`, `exact_test_or_assertion_locator`, `execution_command`), runtime (`runtime_evidence`), and production (tracked separately in coverage metrics). Every one is pinned honestly: `test_id` empty on all 96, `result=NOT_EXECUTED`, `runtime_evidence=NOT_OBSERVED`, `confidence=NOT_SCORED`, `bidirectional_trace_state=NOT_ESTABLISHED`. The validator enforces three real rules against this (`doc03_no_unverified_normative_requirements`, `doc03_verification_not_overclaimed`, `doc03_test_id_assertion_locator_consistency`). Coverage metrics do **not** obscure unknown status — `verified_coverage=0.0` in every domain, `accepted_normative_requirements=0`, and each row carries the limitation "Candidate match rate is not requirement coverage." That is exactly right. Requirements are uniquely identified (96/96 distinct) and traceable to source path and line. They are **not atomic in any semantic sense** and not testable — see **R2-08**.

### Document 04 — Lifecycle (8 state rows, 169 transitions, 12 invalid rules)
Dimensions are genuinely orthogonal in design — twelve independent columns (`document_lifecycle_state`, `authority_state`, `adoption_state`, `accession_state`, `activation_state`, `implementation_authority`, `production_authority`, `evidence_status`, `uncertainty_status`, `suspension_state`, `supersession_state`, `custody_state`) — and a reader can cleanly distinguish documentary maturity (`FOUNDER_REVIEW_READY`) from operational activation (`NOT_ACTIVE`). The transition matrix is complete as a 13×13 Cartesian product with 15 permitted edges, each carrying required authority and required evidence, and the permitted path (`CANDIDATE → DRAFT_UNMERGED → FOUNDER_REVIEW_READY → ADOPTED → LOCKED → ACCESSIONED → ACTIVE`) contains no circular authority — each step requires an authority strictly outside the artifact. Invalid combinations are comprehensive in coverage but **vacuously satisfied** (**R2-07**), transitions are **incomplete** for candidate-stage supersession (**R2-09**) and for recovery from rejection (**R2-10**), and the state count is **misstated in the Founder packet** (**R2-02**).

### Document 05 — Founder decision scope and authority (5 rows, 27 columns)
Structurally the best-designed register in the package. Every decision carries scope, `exclusions`, `authority_source`, `permitted_effect`, `authority_expressly_not_granted` (the prohibited-inference field), `conditions_precedent`, `dependencies`, `options_considered`, `evidence_quality`, `unresolved_consequence`, and `expiration_or_review_date` (2026-09-01). `selected_disposition=NO_DISPOSITION_SELECTED` and `authority_granted=NONE_BY_THIS_PACKAGE` on all five, both validator-enforced. `recommended_option=NO_RECOMMENDATION_SELECTED` avoids steering. **No row operates as self-authorizing governance** — each explicitly disclaims authority and routes it to a Founder act outside the package. The defects are in the *content* of two questions (**R2-02**, **R2-03**) and in one scope gap (**R2-14**), not in the frame.

### Document 06 — Findings, risks, exceptions, waivers (9 rows, 40 columns)
The schema is excellent and the data does not exercise it. Waiver controls are all present as fields — `expiration_date`, `renewal_limit`, `renewal_evidence`, `automatic_reopening_upon_expiration`, `silent_permanent_continuation_prohibited=YES`, `compensating_controls`, `monitoring_requirement` — which is a serious answer to "can a waiver quietly become permanent." ## Validation Adequacy

The main validator is **substantially better than its reputation would suggest** and I independently reproduced its result (212 checks, 0 failures, exit 0). It performs genuine work: 141 manifest hash-and-byte-length bindings, manifest-of-manifests binding, foreign-key validation across source/cluster registers, three real Document 03 anti-overclaim assertions, Founder-decision question-text consistency between register and packet, template count and distinct-name checks, and a terminology blocklist. It is stdlib-only, portable, records `python_version` and `platform`, and discloses its own limitation (`Report is stored inside the assessed package`). Both retained reports carry 212 checks and match my run.

**Blind spots, in order of consequence:**

1. **No check reconciles decision-frame prose against the registers it names.** This is what let "thirteen-state" survive into five files (**R2-02**) and FD-004 point at an empty register (**R2-03**).
2. **No negative fixtures.** Every check is exercised only against data that passes. The 12 invalid-state rules have never been observed to fail (**R2-07**).
3. **Self-attestation counted as verification.** `invalid_rules_marked_enforced` checks that a CSV column says `ENFORCED_BY_VALIDATOR`.
4. **Population-completeness is never checked.** Nothing asserts that the external finding set is a subset of Document 06 (**R2-04**), or that classification coverage in Document 06 includes risks and waivers.
5. **Per-document validators are presence tests presented as assurance** (**R2-06**).
6. **Expression matching is by substring** with no unrecognised-expression failure — editing rule text silently disables enforcement.
7. **Semantic quality is out of scope entirely.** Nothing detects that a `requirement_text` is a JSON literal or a markdown table row (**R2-08**).
8. **Dead code**: the `for n in range(3, 11)` loop contains `... if False else 0`, a no-op left in the shipped validator.

**Validator PASS here means:** the package is structurally coherent, cryptographically intact, referentially sound, and free of the specific overclaims it was written to catch. It does **not** mean the decisions are answerable, the findings population is complete, or the requirements are requirements.
